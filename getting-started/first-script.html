    First script#

    Last edited October 20, 2026

    The script below runs the complete SBTK sequence on the shape model of the asteroid Didymos: it defines a body and a known interior (the true model), computes the exact gravity coefficients and moments of inertia of that interior, turns them into measurements by giving them uncertainties and adding noise, estimates the densities of the interior from those measurements, and compares the estimate with the true model. It takes about 15 s.

    Script#

    Replace /path/to/SBTK with the location of the repository and run the script.

    MATLAB script: first_script.m
    run("/path/to/SBTK/sbtk_setup.m")

% 1. Body: shape model, total mass [kg], reference radius of the gravity field [km]
body = sbtk.body.from_mass("Didymos", sbtk.shape.bundled("didymos"), 5.273e11, 0.43);
sbtk.body.summary(body)

% 2. A known interior: a dense core and a low-density surface layer
components = [sbtk.interior.sphere("core", body.centroid_km, 0.170, +450), ...   % center [km]: the center of figure
              sbtk.interior.layer("regolith", 0, 0.012, -900)];        % from 0 to 12 m below the surface
interior = sbtk.interior.create(components);

% 3. The true model and its exact gravity coefficients (degrees 1-4) and moments of inertia
[exact, truth] = sbtk.data.simulate(body, interior, 4, "first_script");

% 4. Measurements: 1-sigma uncertainties, then one realization of noise drawn from them
unc = sbtk.data.uncertainties([sbtk.units.offset_to_degree1(0.002, 0.43), ...   % degree 1: 2 m in the center of mass
                               1e-4, 2e-4, 4e-4], ...                           % degrees 2, 3, 4
                              2e-3, ...                                         % each inertia element / (M R_ref^2)
                              0.02);                                            % libration ratio
obs = sbtk.data.add_noise(sbtk.data.set_uncertainties(exact, unc), 1);
sbtk.data.summary(obs)

% 5. Estimate the relative densities of the same components (the values given above are not used)
fit = sbtk.invert.solve(body, components, obs);
sbtk.invert.summary(fit)

% 6. Compare the estimate with the true model
cmp = sbtk.compare.with_truth(body, truth, fit);
sbtk.compare.summary(cmp)
sbtk.plot.truth_vs_estimate(body, truth, fit);

    What each step does#

    1. Body. sbtk.shape.bundled reads the DART shape model of Didymos (version 3, 9.3 m sampling, 49,152 facets) in km. sbtk.body.from_mass attaches the total mass M=5.273×1011 kg and the reference radius Rref=0.43 km of the coefficients; the bulk density M/V follows from the shape volume. The center of figure is 57 m from the origin of the shape-model file, which is not the body's center (Coordinate systems and origins).
    2. Interior. The core is a sphere of radius 170 m centered at the center of figure, body.centroid_km, 450 kg/m³ denser than the background; positions of spheres, ellipsoids and tori are shape-model coordinates, and the origin of this file is 57 m from the center of figure (Where to place components). The regolith is everything within 12 m of the surface, measured along rays from the center of mass, 900 kg/m³ lighter than the background. The background density is not given: it is set so that the total mass equals M (Density distributions).
    3. True model. sbtk.data.simulate computes the fully normalized gravity coefficients of degree 1 to 4, the six inertia elements and the libration ratio of this interior, about its center of mass (Gravity and moment calculations). It returns the true model (truth) and its exact values (exact), with no uncertainties: none are involved in the forward model. Degree-1 coefficients give the center of mass (Gravity coefficients).
    4. Measurements. sbtk.data.set_uncertainties gives every coefficient the 1σ of its degree and every inertia quantity its 1σ; sbtk.data.add_noise adds one Gaussian realization drawn from them with seed 1 (Measurement sets).
    5. Estimation. sbtk.invert.solve fits the background density and the relative density of each component to the 24 coefficients and, with the standard settings of sbtk.invert.options, to Ixx, Iyy and Izz, with the total mass as a constraint (Density estimation). The reduced χ2 of 1.11 with p=0.32 means that the model fits the data within their uncertainties (Goodness of fit).
    6. Comparison. sbtk.compare.with_truth evaluates both densities throughout the body. R2=0.37 is the fraction of the variance of the true density that the estimate explains; Rmax2=1 because the estimate uses the true geometry. Each component's estimate is within 2.1σ of its true density. The core is not resolved (its relative density is smaller than its 1σ): a core concentric with the body changes the field much as the background and the regolith layer do, so at these uncertainties the three are hard to tell apart. An off-center core, or more precise inertia measurements, separate them better (Tutorial ex08).
