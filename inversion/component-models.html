    Models with components#

    Last edited October 30, 2026

    A model with components describes the interior as a background density and a few bodies of uniform relative density: a core, a surface layer, a torus, spheres, ellipsoids, sectors (Density distributions). Each is chosen to account for part of the measured anomaly. Such a model has few parameters and can say precisely where a core is and how dense; its risk is the choice of shapes, which the measurements test only through the fit.

    What is estimated#

    Quantity How Page
    The background density and each component's relative density linear least squares with the mass constraint and the bounds 0≤ρ≤ρmax Density estimation
    The center of a sphere, ellipsoid or torus, the axis of a torus or ellipsoid Gauss–Newton with a trust region, a priori constraints and limits Estimated geometry
    Sizes: a radius, the ring and tube radii of a torus, a layer's thickness a grid search, densities solved at every combination Searching the geometry
    A component's place along a line, such as the center-of-mass offset the χ2 profile, then Gauss–Newton from its lowest point Degree 1 and the core
    Compact masses where the residuals ask for them added one at a time, each kept if it passes the likelihood-ratio test Searching the geometry

    Choosing the components#

    The anomaly C − C_CD, the measured coefficients minus those of the same shape at constant density, says what to try (Reading the gravity coefficients):

    The measurements show Try
    the center of mass off the center of figure (degree 1) a core along the offset: dense on that side, or light on the opposite side
    every coefficient scaled by the same factor a central mass (dense below 1, light above)
    a scaling that falls or rises with degree a surface layer
    only even zonal terms (J2, J4) changed a torus or ring about the spin axis
    C¯22, S¯22 changed an elongated core, or bodies at the ends of an equatorial axis
    the moments of inertia larger (or smaller) than at constant density mass farther from (closer to) the center: a dense surface or a light center
    every order of degrees 2 to 4 changed something off center and not symmetric: compare with a sector grid or point masses

    Fitting a model#

    MATLAB
    cof = body.centroid_km;
rm  = (3*body.volume_km3/(4*pi))^(1/3);                     % mean radius
core  = sbtk.interior.sphere("core", cof, 0.40*rm, 0);
torus = sbtk.interior.torus("torus", 0.95*rm, 0.15*rm, 0, 0);
torus.center_km = cof;                                     % about the z-axis through the center of figure
layer = sbtk.interior.layer("regolith", 0, 0.03*rm, 0);
opts = sbtk.invert.options(moi_labels={'Ixx', 'Iyy', 'Izz'});

% place the core by its chi-square profile along the center-of-mass offset
an = sbtk.interpret.anomaly(obs, body, obs.degree);
u  = an.com_offset_km/norm(an.com_offset_km);
sc = sbtk.invert.scan_position(sbtk.invert.setup(body, [core, torus, layer], obs, opts), "core", ...
                               cof + (-0.40:0.025:0.40)'*rm*u);
core.center_km = sc.centers_km(sc.best, :);
core.estimate_position = true;                             % then Gauss-Newton from there
fit = sbtk.invert.solve(body, [core, torus, layer], obs, opts);
fprintf("reduced chi-square %.2f, AIC %.1f\n", fit.chi2_reduced, fit.chi2 + 2*(fit.n_obs - fit.dof))

    projects/hera_hlp6/analyze_rob.m fits the models of rob_candidates.m to every ROB package in this way, from a core at the center of figure to models with four or five components, runs two searches of the geometry (Searching the geometry), and ranks them all by AIC. The runner rob<n>_components.m of each package fits the model of lowest AIC (Code of the ROB solutions).

    Rules#

    • Start simple, add one component at a time, and keep it only if it lowers the AIC: it must lower χ2 by more than 2 for each parameter it adds, a size chosen on a grid included (Goodness of fit).
    • Place before refining. Degree 1 fixes only the product of a core's mass excess and its offset: a dense core on one side and a light core on the other give the same degree 1. Gauss–Newton started at the center of figure can end on the wrong side. On synthetic interiors with a lone core, a core, torus and layer model started from the lowest point of the χ2 profile found the core within 10 m in 83% of them, started from the center in 17% (What the study found).
    • Check that the geometry converged. Where a density and a position trade against each other, as a dense core's density and its offset do, Gauss–Newton can need more than a hundred steps, as for the core of ROB-DATA4. fit.geometry_converged false means the opts.max_iter steps (200 by default) ran out first, and sbtk.invert.summary says so: raise it and fit again.
    • A density at a bound (fit.at_bound) means the shape or place is wrong, or the component too small for the mass the measurements want there.
    • A density within 1σ of zero (fit.resolved false) is not resolved: the component can be dropped.
    • Free geometry can absorb structure the model lacks, and can have several minima. Compare fits started from different places, and give the geometry the a priori uncertainty it really has (Estimated geometry).
    • Test on what was not fitted. A model that fits the measured degrees can still be wrong inside: compare it with a solution that assumes no shapes (point masses), and with data it was not fitted to (Fitting part of the coefficients).
