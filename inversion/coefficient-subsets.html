    Fitting part of the coefficients#

    Last edited October 27, 2026

    A model can be fitted to some of the coefficients and tested on the others. This answers two questions: which coefficients determine which parts of a model (can degree 1 and J2 alone place a core?), and whether a model fitted to the well-measured low degrees predicts the higher ones, which is a test on data it was not fitted to.

    Functions#

    Step Function
    Keep some coefficients sbtk.data.select_coefficients(obs, labels), sbtk.data.select_degree(obs, L)
    Fit them sbtk.invert.setup, sbtk.invert.run (or sbtk.invert.solve)
    Predict every coefficient from the fit sbtk.invert.predict(fit, body, obs) with the full set
    Map the residuals of several fits sbtk.plot.coefficient_residuals
    Compare anomalies coefficient by coefficient sbtk.plot.anomaly with sbtk.interpret.anomaly of each fit

    sbtk.invert.predict evaluates the fitted model (its components with their fitted geometry and densities) for every measurement of the full set: for the fitted rows it gives the fit's own predictions, for the others new ones. It reports the χ2 of each group with its own covariance (pred.chi2_fitted, pred.chi2_other). The fit and the full set must give the same measured center of mass, so keep degree 1 in every subset of data about a fixed point; otherwise the model's layers and sectors would be measured from another point and sbtk:invert:predictOrigin stops the prediction.

    A subset with fewer coefficients than free parameters leaves the fit underdetermined: fit.determined is false, the solution is one of many, and its χ2 is 0 by construction. A core alone has four unknowns (its relative density and three coordinates), which degree 1 and J2 determine exactly; a core, a torus and a layer have six.

    MATLAB fragment. Requires the variables defined in Reading the coefficients of a hidden interior.
    part = sbtk.data.select_coefficients(obs, ["C10" "C11" "S11" "C20"]);   % degree 1 and J2
core = sbtk.interior.sphere("core", cm.center_km, 0.15, 0);
core.estimate_position = true;
fit1 = sbtk.invert.solve(body, core, part, opts);
pred1 = sbtk.invert.predict(fit1, body, obs);
fit3 = sbtk.invert.solve(body, model, sbtk.data.select_degree(obs, 3), opts);
pred3 = sbtk.invert.predict(fit3, body, obs);
fprintf('degree 1 + J2: chi2 per coefficient of the others %.3g\n', pred1.chi2_other/pred1.n_other);
fprintf('degrees 1-3:   chi2 per coefficient of degrees 4-5 %.3g\n', pred3.chi2_other/pred3.n_other);
fig = sbtk.plot.coefficient_residuals({pred1, pred3}, ["core, degree 1 + J2", "three components, degrees 1-3"]);

    How well the fitted coefficients are matched#

    A fit to part of the coefficients has to be judged on two groups: the coefficients it was fitted to, which it matches by construction as well as its parameters allow, and the others, which it predicts. sbtk.plot.coefficient_residuals shows both for several fits at once, one row per fit and one column per measurement, with the fitted cells outlined; sbtk.plot.anomaly shows the anomaly of a few fits against the measured one, filled where fitted and open where predicted.

    tests/studies/coefficient_reading_figures.m does this for one of the study's true interiors (the first of the family "core, torus, layer": a core of +776 kg/m³ and radius 121 m, 54 m from the center of figure, a torus of −652 kg/m³ and a layer of −421 kg/m³), with noise at the Hera precision, fitted with the three-component model of guessed sizes to each of the eight sets, the core placed by its χ2 profile and Gauss–Newton:

    • With 4, 5 or 6 coefficients for 6 unknowns, the fit matches its coefficients exactly (χ2 per coefficient 10⁻¹⁵ to 10⁻²): the row is white where it is outlined. That says nothing about the model; the other coefficients do (34.5, 9.9 and 12.2 per coefficient), and the core ends 29 to 187 m from the true one.
    • From degrees 1–2 the fit has more coefficients than unknowns and its own χ2 rises (1.9 to 3.3 per coefficient): the model with these sizes cannot match the coefficients to their 1σ. J4 brings the prediction of the others from 16.8 to 3.3; degrees 1–4 predict degree 5 at 1.9, and the core is then 9 m from the true one.
    • S¯22 is several σ off in every fit. The true interior has the same three kinds of component; the model's, of other sizes, do not make its S¯22 at any densities and core position.

    Results of the study#

    What the study found reports the 15,840 fits of tests/studies/coefficient_reading_study.m. For the choice of coefficients:

    • A core that is alone is determined by degree 1 and J2, and the fit predicts every other coefficient as well as the noise allows (1.0 per coefficient). More coefficients do not improve its position (4–5 m at the Hera precision).
    • A core with a torus and a layer needs J4: degree 1 + J2 + J4 predicts the others at 3.8 per coefficient against 21.5 without J4; degrees 1–2 + J3 + J4 at 3.1; degrees 1–4 predict degree 5 at 1.5. J3 adds little and, with noise, moves the core to fit the noise.
    • Degrees 1–2 alone are not a test: 63% of those fits are consistent with their coefficients and still predict the others at 16 per coefficient; for interiors with no core at all, 60% and 45.
    • The fixed-point iteration that moves the core to match degree 1 (sbtk.invert.recenter) converges in about 3 steps for a core alone, and not at all when other components can match degree 1 themselves.
