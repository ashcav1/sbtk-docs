    Goodness of fit and model comparison#

    Chi-square test#

    For a fit with residuals 𝐫=𝐲pred−𝐲 and measurement covariance Σ:

    Quantity Definition Field
    χ2 𝐫𝖳Σ−1𝐫, with the full covariance fit.chi2
    Effective number of parameters peff=tr(JdPJd𝖳), the trace of the data resolution matrix (Jd whitened measurement Jacobian, P parameter covariance); it accounts for fixed parameters and regularization
    Degrees of freedom ν=n−peff fit.dof
    Reduced χ2 χ2/ν; close to 1 when the model and the stated uncertainties are correct fit.chi2_reduced
    p-value P(χν2≥χ2), the probability that a correct model gives a χ2 at least as large, computed as the upper regularized incomplete gamma function Q(ν/2,χ2/2) fit.p_value
    Consistent p≥0.05: the model is consistent with the measurements at the 5% significance level fit.consistent

    sbtk.invert.goodness_of_fit computes the last three from χ2 and ν. When ν≤10−6 (at least as many parameters as measurements) the test is undefined: the reduced χ2 and the p-value are NaN and consistent is false.

    Homogeneous body#

    sbtk.invert.homogeneous fits a body of uniform density M/V to the same measurements; it has no free parameters (ν=n). If this fit is already consistent with the measurements, the data do not require internal structure at their precision.

    Normalized residuals#

    (ypred−y)/σ for every measurement (fit.normalized_residual, sbtk.plot.residuals). For a correct model about 68% lie within ±1. Large residuals concentrated in one group indicate structure that the model lacks: in the degree-1 rows, a model that cannot place the center of mass; at one degree, structure at the corresponding scale.

    When a fit is rejected (p<0.05), its densities and uncertainties describe the best model of the chosen form, not the body. At high measurement precision, small structures that a component model omits, such as a random density field of a few percent of the bulk density or a boulder tens of meters across, can be enough to reject an otherwise correct model (Hera blind test).

    Comparison with a true model#

    In synthetic tests the true model is known. sbtk.compare.with_truth(body, truth, fit) evaluates both densities at the centroids of 24 radial shells of every facet cone (1,179,648 points for the 49,152-facet Didymos model), each weighted by the shell volume wi, with each model's layers and sectors measured from its own origin. It returns (cmp):

    R2=1−∑iwi(ρest,i−ρtrue,i)2∑iwi(ρtrue,i−ρ¯true)2,

    the fraction of the volume-weighted variance of the true density about its mean that the estimate explains: 1 for an exact estimate, 0 for one no better than a uniform density, negative for a worse one.

    • Rmax2: the largest R2 attainable with the same components, from a weighted least-squares fit of the true density by the background and the components. It measures the limit set by the choice of components; R2 close to Rmax2 means the measurements determined the densities as well as that geometry allows.
    • The volume-weighted RMS error and the standard deviation of the true density.
    • For each component present in both models: estimate, 1σ, true density and |estimate−true|/σ.

    For many fits of the same components against one true model, sbtk.compare.reference samples the true model once and sbtk.compare.evaluate compares each fit in milliseconds. sbtk.compare.summary prints a comparison and sbtk.plot.comparison plots it.

    Comparison of density grids#

    Any two models can be compared on a common cubic grid, independently of how they are parametrized. sbtk.exchange.density_grid(body, model, spacing_km) evaluates a model at cell centers that lie at integer multiples of the spacing along the axes of the shape-model frame; cells whose center is inside the shape model (tested along rays from the center of figure) are kept, so all models of a body have the same cells (grid). sbtk.compare.grids(truth, estimate) compares the cells present in both grids (gridcmp): R2 with equal weights, RMS and mean error, and the fraction of cells whose error is at most twice the 1σ of the estimate. Both grids must have the same spacing (sbtk:compare:gridSpacing). This is how solutions in exchange packages are compared with interior-model packages.

    Calibration of the uncertainties#

    The uncertainties of a fit can be checked with repeated noise realizations: if they are correct, the standard deviation of (ρ^−ρtrue)/σ over many realizations is close to 1.

    MATLAB script: Calibration over noise realizations
    run("/path/to/SBTK/sbtk_setup.m")
body = sbtk.body.from_mass("Didymos", sbtk.shape.bundled("didymos"), 5.273e11, 0.43);
unc  = sbtk.data.uncertainties([sbtk.units.offset_to_degree1(0.002, 0.43), 1e-4, 2e-4, 4e-4], 2e-3, 0.02);
components = [sbtk.interior.sphere("core", [0 0 0], 0.17, +450), sbtk.interior.layer("regolith", 0, 0.012, -900)];
[exact, truth] = sbtk.data.simulate(body, sbtk.interior.create(components), 4, "calibration");
exact = sbtk.data.set_uncertainties(exact, unc);

prob = sbtk.invert.setup(body, components, sbtk.data.add_noise(exact, 1), sbtk.invert.options());
z = zeros(30, 3);
for k = 1:30
    f = sbtk.invert.run(sbtk.invert.replace_data(prob, sbtk.data.add_noise(exact, 100 + k)));
    z(k, :) = ([f.rho_bg_kgm3, [f.components.drho_kgm3]] - [truth.rho_bg_kgm3, [components.drho_kgm3]]) ...
              ./[f.rho_bg_sigma_kgm3, f.drho_sigma_kgm3];
end
fprintf("standard deviation of (estimate - truth)/sigma: %.2f\n", std(z(:)))
