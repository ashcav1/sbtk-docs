    Sector grids#

    Last edited October 28, 2026

    A sector grid divides the whole body into blocks bounded by latitude, longitude and depth, each with its own density. Like point masses it assumes no shapes, but its blocks are large: tens of them instead of thousands of points, each reaching from one radial fraction of the way to the surface to another. It shows in which directions and at which depths the body is denser or lighter than its bulk density.

    Building a grid#

    sbtk.interior.sector_grid(frac_edges, lat_edges_deg, lon_edges_deg) returns one sector per radial shell, latitude band and longitude band (Density distributions). Radial edges are fractions of the distance from the expansion origin to the surface along each ray, so every shell follows the shape. The grids of the ROB pages:

    Grid Radial edges Latitude edges Longitude edges Sectors
    1 × 4 × 8 0, 1 −90°, −45°, 0°, 45°, 90° every 45° from −180° 32, each from the center to the surface
    2 × 4 × 8 0, 0.5, 1 the same the same 64: an inner and an outer shell

    Sectors near the poles are smaller than those at the equator, and inner sectors smaller than outer ones; their volumes enter the fit through their integrals.

    The fit#

    A sector grid is fitted like any model with components (Density estimation), with two differences:

    • The background is held at the bulk density (opts.background = "bulk"). The sectors fill the body, so the background and a common change of every sector density are the same thing.
    • Tikhonov regularization penalizes the relative densities, λ∑c(Δρc/ρ¯)2, so that the measurements, which determine fewer combinations than there are sectors, do not produce large alternating densities. The bounds 0≤ρ≤ρmax still apply. The effective number of parameters is the trace of the data resolution matrix (fit.dof).

    Choosing the regularization parameter#

    Rule λ Use
    lowest AIC the lowest χ2+2peff: analyze_rob.m scans log10λ from −3 to 8 in steps of 0.25, then refines by golden-section search comparing sector grids with other kinds of model on equal terms (Goodness of fit)
    smoothest consistent the largest with p≥0.05 (bisection on log10λ) the least structure the measurements require
    discrepancy principle sbtk.invert.choose_regularization: the largest with χ2≤max(n,χmin2+2n) noisy measurements of known precision (Regularization)
    MATLAB
    sectors = sbtk.interior.sector_grid([0 1], -90:45:90, -180:45:180);   % 1 x 4 x 8
prob = sbtk.invert.setup(body, sectors, obs, sbtk.invert.options(background="bulk", moi_labels={'Ixx', 'Iyy', 'Izz'}));
aic = @(e) aic_at(prob, e);
g = -3:0.25:8;
[~, k] = min(arrayfun(aic, g));
prob.opts.regularization = 10^g(k);
fit = sbtk.invert.run(prob);

function a = aic_at(prob, log_lambda)
prob.opts.regularization = 10^log_lambda;
f = sbtk.invert.run(prob);
a = f.chi2 + 2*(f.n_obs - f.dof);
end

    Reading the result#

    • Each sector's relative density and its 1σ (fit.components(c).drho_kgm3, fit.drho_sigma_kgm3). The regularization correlates neighboring sectors, and a sector whose relative density is within 1σ of zero is not resolved.
    • What a grid can represent. A sector reaches from its inner to its outer radial fraction. One shell cannot put a dense core under a normal surface or a light ring 300 m out under a normal crust: on ROB-DATA3, a light ring about the spin axis, both grids stay far from the measurements (ROB-DATA3). Where the anomaly is spread over a direction and its depth, a grid fits where compact components cannot: on ROB-DATA2, a tilted, off-center anomaly, it is the best solution (ROB-DATA2).
    • The edges are a choice. A structure that straddles an edge is split between two sectors. Compare grids with different edges, or with point masses (Point masses), before reading a single sector.
