    Uncertainties and the expansion point#

    Last edited October 22, 2026

    A gravity field can be written about any point, and its coefficients depend on that point. So do their uncertainties. The Hera radio-science (RSE) team gives the uncertainties of a field about the center of mass; the HLP6 packages give coefficients about the origin of the shape model file, 56 m from the center of mass of Didymos in UCB-DATA1. This lesson starts from what a coefficient measures, derives how the coefficients and their uncertainties change when the expansion point moves, works the Didymos and Dimorphos cases by hand, and ends with the intuition and what to do.

    1. What a coefficient measures#

    A coefficient of degree l is a moment of the mass distribution of order l, taken about the expansion point. With x,y,z measured from that point, M the mass and R the reference radius, the coefficients of degree 0 to 2 are (unnormalized):

    Coefficient Moment Meaning
    C00=1 1M∫ρdV the mass
    C10, C11, S11 1MR∫ρzdV, 1MR∫ρxdV, 1MR∫ρydV the center of mass from the point, z¯/R, x¯/R, y¯/R
    C20 1MR2∫ρ(z2−12(x2+y2))dV flattening along z
    C22 14MR2∫ρ(x2−y2)dV elongation along x

    The fully normalized coefficients of the packages are C¯lm=Clm/Nlm, with N10=N11=3, N20=5 and N22=5/12 (Gravity coefficients). About the center of mass, degree 1 is zero by definition; about any other point it gives where the center of mass is.

    2. Moving the point: a warm-up in one dimension#

    Take mass along a line and its second moment about the origin, ∫ρx2dx. About a point at x=h instead:

    ∫ρ(x−h)2dx=∫ρx2dx−2h∫ρxdx+h2∫ρdx.

    The second moment about the new point is the old second moment, plus the old first moment times h, plus the old zeroth moment (the mass) times h2. This is the parallel-axis theorem. Two things follow, and they hold for every degree:

    • a moment about the new point mixes in the moments of lower order about the old one, weighted by powers of the offset; never higher ones;
    • if a lower moment is uncertain, the higher moments about the new point inherit that uncertainty, multiplied by the offset (the lever arm).

    3. The rules for degrees 1 and 2#

    Let h=(hx,hy,hz) be the new expansion point minus the old one, so that the coordinates about the new point are x−hx, y−hy, z−hz. Putting these into the moments of section 1:

    Degree 1 picks up only the mass:

    C10′=C10−hzR,C11′=C11−hxR,S11′=S11−hyR.

    Degree 2 picks up degree 1 and the mass. Expanding (z−hz)2−12((x−hx)2+(y−hy)2) and (x−hx)2−(y−hy)2:

    C20′=C20−2hzRC10+hxRC11+hyRS11+hz2−12(hx2+hy2)R2, C22′=C22−hx2RC11+hy2RS11+hx2−hy24R2.

    In fully normalized coefficients (divide each side by its Nlm):

    C¯20′=C¯20+35(−2hzRC¯10+hxRC¯11+hyRS¯11)+const, C¯22′=C¯22+35(−hxRC¯11+hyRS¯11)+const.

    The constants hold only h and C00=1. For every degree at once the rule is linear, c′=Tc, with T the translation matrix of solid harmonics (sbtk.internal.sh_translation): degree l about the new point is a combination of degrees 0 to l about the old one, with powers of h/R.

    4. From values to uncertainties#

    A linear map carries a covariance K to K′=TKT𝖳. For one coefficient this means: the variance of a sum of independent terms is the sum of their variances, each with the square of its weight, and a constant adds nothing. The offset h is known exactly (the shape model file is agreed) and C00=1, so only the uncertain coefficients count. With uncorrelated inputs:

    σ2(C¯1m′)=σ2(C¯1m)(degree 1 does not change) σ2(C¯20′)=σ2(C¯20)+125(hzR)2σ2(C¯10)+35(hxR)2σ2(C¯11)+35(hyR)2σ2(S¯11) σ2(C¯22′)=σ2(C¯22)+95(hxR)2σ2(C¯11)+95(hyR)2σ2(S¯11)

    The 1σ of degree 1 is the uncertainty of the center of mass: σx=3Rσ(C¯11), σy=3Rσ(S¯11), σz=3Rσ(C¯10). With it the added terms read as a lever arm times a position error, over R2; for example the z term of C¯20 is 25hzσz/R2.

    If the inputs are correlated, each pair adds twice its covariance times the two weights; TKT𝖳 includes that. The RSE .tab files give only one 1σ per coefficient, so the examples below treat them as uncorrelated, as sbtk.data.use_uncertainties does.

    5. Example: the zonal coefficient of degree 2 of Didymos (UCB-DATA1)#

    The package gives degree 1 about the origin of the file; it puts the center of mass at c=(15.03, 42.27, −33.98) m. The RSE field is about the center of mass, so the expansion point moves from the center of mass to the origin: h=−c=(−15.03, −42.27, 33.98) m, and with R=410 m, h/R=(−0.0367, −0.1031, 0.0829). The RSE 1σ, converted to R=410 m (factor (430/410)l):

    σ(C¯20) σ(C¯10) σ(C¯11) σ(S¯11)
    RSE, R = 410 m 8.70 × 10⁻⁶ 1.378 × 10⁻⁴ (σz = 9.8 cm) 1.240 × 10⁻⁵ (σx = 0.9 cm) 1.304 × 10⁻⁵ (σy = 0.9 cm)

    The terms of σ(C¯20′):

    Term Value
    own, σ(C¯20) 8.70 × 10⁻⁶
    z: 12/5(0.0829)(1.378×10−4) 1.77 × 10⁻⁵
    x: 3/5(0.0367)(1.240×10−5) 3.5 × 10⁻⁷
    y: 3/5(0.1031)(1.304×10−5) 1.04 × 10⁻⁶
    total, sum of squares 1.97 × 10⁻⁵, 2.27 times the RSE value

    The z term dominates: the 34 m height of the origin above the center of mass is the lever arm, and the center of mass is least certain along z (9.8 cm against 0.9 cm in x and y).

    6. Example: the sectoral coefficient of degree 2 of Dimorphos (UCB-DATA4)#

    The center of mass is at c=(−1.11, −2.26, −0.25) m from the origin of the Dimorphos file, so h=(1.11, 2.26, 0.25) m and, with R=100 m, h/R=(0.0111, 0.0226, 0.0025).

    σ(C¯22) σ(C¯11) σ(S¯11)
    RSE 4.04 × 10⁻⁶ 2.462 × 10⁻³ (σx = 0.43 m) 3.380 × 10⁻³ (σy = 0.59 m)
    Term Value
    own, σ(C¯22) 4.04 × 10⁻⁶
    x: 35(0.0111)(2.462×10−3) 3.67 × 10⁻⁵
    y: 35(0.0226)(3.380×10−3) 1.03 × 10⁻⁴
    total 1.09 × 10⁻⁴, 27 times the RSE value

    The offset is only 2.5 m, but the center of mass of Dimorphos is known to about half a meter, and C¯22 itself is known very well (4×10−6). A small lever arm on a large position error swamps a small own uncertainty.

    The other coefficients, worked the same way (answers in section 10): Dimorphos C¯20 grows 1.60 times, Didymos C¯22 only 1.003 times, because C¯22 couples only to x and y, where the center of mass of Didymos is known to a centimeter.

    7. Example: degree 1 does not change#

    C¯10′=C¯10−hz/(3R), and hz is known exactly. For UCB-DATA1 the value goes from about zero (about the center of mass) to −33.98 m/(3·410 m)=−0.0478, the C¯10 in the package, while the 1σ stays 1.378×10−4. Degree 1 is the position of the center of mass; measuring it from a different fixed point changes the number, not how well it is known.

    8. Example: the moments of inertia#

    The inertia elements move by the parallel-axis theorem, Iij(origin)=Iij(c.m.)+M(|c|2δij−cicj), so Ixx(origin)=Ixx(c.m.)+M(cy2+cz2). An error in c changes it by 2M(cyδcy+czδcz), and in units of MR2 the added 1σ is

    2R2(cyσy)2+(czσz)2.

    For Dimorphos (UCB-DATA4): 2(100 m)2(2.26·0.585)2+(0.25·0.418)2 m2=2.65×10−4, against the RSE 1σ of Ixx, 1.1081×104 kg km² /(MR2)=4.10×10−4 (the file labels it kg m²; Didymos and Dimorphos values): the total is 4.89×10−4, 19% more than the RSE 1σ. For Didymos the added term is 4×10−5 against an RSE 1σ of 2.8×10−2, which is negligible. (The inertia errors are also correlated with degree 1, which SBTK, keeping inertia and coefficients uncorrelated, cannot carry; it adds the variance only.)

    9. The intuition#

    • Coefficients describe the mass as seen from a point. About the center of mass, the RSE measures the shape of the field very well and the position of the center of mass separately. About another point, every coefficient also encodes where the center of mass sits relative to that point, so the position error enters them.
    • The offset is a lever arm. As in the parallel-axis theorem, the error a position uncertainty causes grows with the distance: no offset, no added uncertainty. That is why the field is best described about the center of mass.
    • It reaches every higher degree. Degree l mixes in all lower degrees with powers of h/R, so the uncertainty of the center of mass reaches degree 2, 3 and above, with weights that fall as h/R is small.
    • It matters most where a coefficient is known best. A coefficient whose own 1σ is tiny (Dimorphos C¯22) is overwhelmed; one with a large own 1σ hardly changes.
    • Nothing is lost. K′=TKT𝖳 has correlations between degree 1 and the rest. A fit that uses the full covariance gives the same answer about the origin as about the center of mass. The mistake is to keep the RSE 1σ, which belong to the center of mass, for coefficients about the origin: that claims those coefficients are known up to 27 times better than they are, and a fit then weights them too much.

    10. What to do, and answers#

    To weight data about a fixed point with uncertainties given about the center of mass, do one of these two; they give the same fit:

    1. move the covariance to the point of the data, K′=TKT𝖳: sbtk.data.use_uncertainties does it when the reference set is about another point, and projects/hera_hlp6/hlp6_uncertainties.m gives the RSE field the center of mass the package's degree 1 gives;
    2. move the coefficients to that center of mass (sbtk.data.expand_about) and use the RSE 1σ there.

    The numbers of sections 5 to 8 can be reproduced with the formulas of section 4:

    MATLAB
    R = 0.41;  h = [-15.03 -42.27 33.98]/1e3;                       % Didymos, UCB-DATA1 [km]
s20 = 8.70e-6;  s10 = 1.378e-4;  s11 = 1.240e-5;  t11 = 1.304e-5;  % RSE 1-sigma at R = 0.41 km
c20 = sqrt(s20^2 + 12/5*(h(3)/R)^2*s10^2 + 3/5*(h(1)/R)^2*s11^2 + 3/5*(h(2)/R)^2*t11^2);
fprintf('Didymos C20: %.3g, %.2f times the RSE value\n', c20, c20/s20)          % 1.97e-05, 2.27
R = 0.10;  h = [1.11 2.26 0.25]/1e3;                            % Dimorphos, UCB-DATA4 [km]
s22 = 4.04e-6;  s11 = 2.462e-3;  t11 = 3.380e-3;
c22 = sqrt(s22^2 + 9/5*(h(1)/R)^2*s11^2 + 9/5*(h(2)/R)^2*t11^2);
fprintf('Dimorphos C22: %.3g, %.1f times the RSE value\n', c22, c22/s22)        % 1.09e-04, 27.0

    and for any package, with the full covariance, by SBTK:

    MATLAB
    [obs, body] = sbtk.exchange.read_data("UCB-DATA1-Didymos");
addpath(fullfile(sbtk.root, "projects", "hera_hlp6"));
obs = hlp6_uncertainties(obs, body);      % RSE covariance moved from the center of mass to the file origin

    Exercises. Answers below each.

    1. Dimorphos C¯20 (UCB-DATA4). With σ(C¯20)=5.09×10−5, σ(C¯10)=2.41×10−3 and the rest from section 6: terms z 9.3×10−6, x 2.12×10−5, y 5.92×10−5; total 8.14×10−5, 1.60 times the RSE value.
    2. Didymos C¯22 (UCB-DATA1). With σ(C¯22)=2.645×10−5: terms x 6.1×10−7, y 1.8×10−6; total 2.652×10−5, 1.003 times: the center of mass of Didymos is known to 0.9 cm in x and y.
    3. How high above the center of mass must the expansion point of Didymos be for σ(C¯20) to double? Doubling means the added variance is three times the own: $\tfrac{12}{5}(h_z/R)^2\sigma^2(\bar C_{10}) = 3\sigma^2(\bar C_{20})$, so $h_z = R\,\sqrt3\,\sigma(\bar C_{20})/(\sqrt{12/5}\,\sigma(\bar C_{10})) = 410\ \text{m} \times 0.0706 \approx$ 29 m. The origin of the file is 34 m above it, hence a little more than double.
    4. Why does σ(C¯10) not change? Its only new term is hz/(3R) times C00=1, and both are exact (section 7).
