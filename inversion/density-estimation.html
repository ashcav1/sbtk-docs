    Density estimation#

    Last edited October 22, 2026

    The inversion estimates the background density and the relative densities of a set of components from a measurement set, and optionally parts of their geometry: the position of spheres, ellipsoids and tori and the axis of ellipsoids and tori (Estimated geometry).

    Procedure#

    sbtk.invert.setup(body, components, obs, opts) builds the geometry about obs.origin_km and the design matrix once (prob); sbtk.invert.run(prob) solves. sbtk.invert.solve does both. Reusing prob is faster when the same problem is solved more than once:

    • with other settings that do not select measurements (prob.opts.regularization, bounds, positions);
    • for a homogeneous body (sbtk.invert.homogeneous);
    • for other realizations of the same measurements (sbtk.invert.replace_data).

    Estimation problem#

    Let 𝐲 be the n measurements (the selected coefficients followed by the inertia rows named in opts.moi_labels) with covariance Σ=LL𝖳. The predictions are linear in the densities:

    𝐲pred=ρbg𝐠body+∑c=1ncΔρc𝐠c,

    where 𝐠body and 𝐠c are the measurements of a unit density in the whole body and in component c (prob.gbody, prob.G). The unknowns 𝐱=(ρbg,Δρ1,…,Δρnc) minimize

    \lVertL−1(𝐲pred−𝐲)\rVert2+λ∑c(Δρcρ¯)2

    subject to

    • the mass constraint ρbgV+∑cΔρcVc=M, with Vc the volume of component c inside the body and M = obs.mass_kg;
    • the bounds 0≤ρbg≤ρmax and 0≤ρbg+Δρc≤ρmax for every component, with ρmax = opts.rho_max_kgm3;
    • fixed parameters: with opts.background = "bulk" the background is held at the bulk density ρ¯=M/V; components with component.estimate_density false keep their relative density.

    λ is the Tikhonov parameter opts.regularization. The problem is solved with lsqlin, first with the active-set algorithm and, if that does not converge, with the interior-point algorithm; if neither converges the run stops with sbtk:invert:solver.

    Estimated geometry#

    Besides the densities, an inversion can estimate the geometry of components. The geometry parameters are listed by sbtk.internal.geometry_parameters:

    Field of the component Components Parameters
    component.estimate_position = true sphere, ellipsoid, torus the three center coordinates, km (<name>_x, _y, _z)
    component.estimate_position = [false false true] sphere, ellipsoid, torus only the coordinates of the mask, for example the height of a torus
    component.estimate_axis = true torus the direction of its axis: two tilts about its own first and second axes, deg (<name>_tilt1, _tilt2)
    component.estimate_axis = true ellipsoid its orientation: the two tilts of its third axis and the turn about it, deg (<name>_tilt1, _tilt2, _turn)

    Setting either field on a component without a center or an axis stops the inversion with sbtk:invert:geometryParameter. The geometry is found by Gauss–Newton iteration:

    • Each step linearizes the measurements in the geometry parameters and solves the bounded problem. The derivatives are central differences of the integrals: 10⁻⁶ km for a sphere inside the body (a point mass), 10⁻⁴ km and 10⁻² deg for components integrated along rays, whose integrals are smooth only on scales larger than the ray spacing (Assumptions and limitations).
    • Trust region. The steps are limited to opts.step_km for a center coordinate and opts.step_deg for a tilt or turn. A step that does not lower the objective is undone and the trust region halved; a successful step restores it.
    • A priori constraints. With opts.position_sigma_km or opts.axis_sigma_deg finite, each center coordinate, tilt or turn has an a priori constraint of that 1σ about its starting value. These are needed when the measurements do not determine all of the geometry, and they keep a component from absorbing structure the model lacks (below).
    • Hard limits. opts.max_shift_km bounds the distance of an estimated center from its start, opts.max_tilt_deg the angle of an estimated axis from its starting direction, and opts.max_turn_deg the turn of an ellipsoid about its axis. Spheres are always kept inside the body.
    • Stopping. Iteration stops after opts.max_iter steps, or earlier when any of these holds:
    • the objective changes by less than 10⁻¹⁰ relative;
    • an accepted step changes every parameter by less than opts.geometry_tol of its step limit;
    • the trust region falls below 10⁻⁶ km.

    Without the step test, a model that lacks structure the data contain keeps lowering χ2 by ever smaller steps. That runs every repetition about the center of mass to its limit.

    For data about the center of mass the fit is repeated about the model's own center of mass (below), and each repetition continues from the geometry of the previous one. The priors and limits always refer to the geometry the components were given.

    Absorbed structure. An estimated geometry can absorb structure the model lacks, so freeing it can lower χ2 while moving the component away from the truth. Example: a Didymos true model has a torus about its spin axis at the center of figure, plus a boulder and a random density field. The data are its exact values weighted by the Hera radio-science precision, in the frame and about the origin of the DART file. An inversion model with a core, the torus (about the z-axis through the center of figure, 3.7° from the true torus axis) and the regolith has a reduced χ2 of 157 (R2=0.49) with fixed geometry. With the torus axis and height estimated (a priori 1σ of 1° and 10 m, limits of 10° and 50 m), χ2 drops to 49 while R2 falls to −1.95: the torus moves 50 m up, to its limit, and tilts 6.9° from z to stand in for the boulder, and the regolith density falls to its lower bound. At this precision such priors are weak. Priors, or limits tight enough to bind, must express how well the geometry is actually known.

    Local minima. A fit whose geometry absorbs structure can have more than one local minimum. The same Didymos fit, in the frame with the center of mass as origin and the spin axes, settled with the torus 41 m above the center of figure (reduced χ2 39) or, with the derivatives taken over smaller steps, 47 m below it (reduced χ2 37); which minimum Gauss–Newton reaches depends on its path. Compare fits started from different geometries before reading a position or an axis as a result.

    sbtk.interior.align_with_spin_axis is the counterpart for making a true model: it sets the axis of tori, ellipsoids or sectors along the model's own axis of the largest moment by fixed-point iteration (Density distributions).

    Uncertainties#

    The covariance of the parameters is the linearized one at the solution, with the mass constraint and the fixed parameters eliminated:

    P=Z(Z𝖳J𝖳JZ)−1Z𝖳,

    where J is the whitened Jacobian of the measurements with the regularization and a priori rows appended, and the columns of Z span the null space of the mass constraint and the fixed parameters. If Z𝖳J𝖳JZ is singular (the measurements do not determine every free parameter), the pseudo-inverse is used and fit.determined is false. The bounds are not included in P; fit.at_bound marks densities at a bound.

    The density of a component and its 1σ are ρbg+Δρc and Pbg,bg+Pcc+2Pbg,c (fit.rho_kgm3, fit.rho_sigma_kgm3). fit.resolved marks components whose relative density exceeds its 1σ. The validation tests check over 40 noise realizations that the standard deviation of (estimate − truth)/σ is within 0.25 of 1, with diagonal and with strongly correlated covariances (Validation).

    Regularization#

    Models with many components, such as a sector grid, or weak data need Tikhonov regularization to keep noise from producing large alternating densities. sbtk.invert.choose_regularization returns the largest λ for which

    χ2≤max(n, χmin2+2n),

    with n the number of measurements and χmin2 the value at λ=10−2 (the discrepancy principle): χ2 may rise by about one standard deviation of its distribution. It searches log10λ∈[−2,8] by bisection with 9 solves; the result is returned, not stored, so set prob.opts.regularization before the final run.

    Data about a fixed point or about the center of mass#

    When obs.coef_about is 'origin', the coefficients, degree 1 included, and the inertia are about the fixed point obs.origin_km: for a package in the frame and about the origin of its shape model file, as the HLP6 makers send, [0 0 0]. Layers and sectors are measured along rays from the center of mass, as in sbtk.data.simulate, so the model's geometry is built about the center of mass the degree-1 coefficients give (fit.origin_km; if that is within 3σ of obs.origin_km, the data do not tell the two apart and obs.origin_km is used). Its coefficients, degree 1 included, and its inertia are moved to obs.origin_km exactly (sbtk.internal.sh_translation, the parallel-axis theorem; fit.com_shift_km is that point minus fit.origin_km). Degree 1 then compares the model's center of mass with the measured one, and no iteration is needed. Measuring layers and sectors from the origin of the file instead, 52 to 57 m from the center of mass of Didymos, changes a sector by about 25% of its volume, and a fit with exactly the components of the true model no longer recovers it (testLayersFromMeasuredCenterOfMass).

    When obs.coef_about is 'center_of_mass', the coefficients of degree 2 and higher are about the body's center of mass, which is known only through its measurement (the degree-1 rows). Expanding the model about the measured center of mass would carry the error of that measurement into the predictions: a translation of the expansion point changes the coefficients of degree l by terms proportional to those of lower degrees (Uncertainties and the expansion point). sbtk.invert.run therefore

    1. keeps the model where it is: its geometry is built about the measured center of mass (fit.origin_km), from which layers and sectors are measured;
    2. moves its predicted coefficients of degree ≥ 2 and inertia to the model's own center of mass, by the exact translation of solid harmonics (sbtk.internal.sh_translation) and the parallel-axis theorem;
    3. iterates (Gauss–Newton, including the dependence of the center of mass on the densities, prob.D) until that center moves by less than 1 mm (fit.frame_iterations, fit.frame_offset_km);
    4. compares the model's center of mass with the measured one through the degree-1 rows.

    Here fit.com_shift_km is the model's center of mass minus fit.origin_km. The tests testCenterOfMassFrameMatchesTheTrueFrame and testPredictionsAboutTheModelCenterOfMass check that the densities agree with an inversion about the true center of mass to within 0.2σ and that the predictions equal a forward model computed from scratch about the fitted center of mass (Validation).

    Settings#

    Setting (opts) Standard value Meaning
    opts.moi_labels {'Ixx','Iyy','Izz'} Inertia rows fitted; {} for gravity coefficients only.
    opts.regularization 0 Tikhonov parameter λ.
    opts.background "estimate" "bulk" holds the background at M/V.
    opts.rho_max_kgm3 4500 Upper bound on every density, kg/m³.
    opts.max_iter 20 Gauss–Newton iterations for estimated geometry.
    opts.step_km 0.010 Trust-region radius of a center coordinate, km.
    opts.step_deg 1 Trust-region radius of an axis tilt or turn, deg.
    opts.position_sigma_km Inf A priori 1σ of center coordinates, km.
    opts.axis_sigma_deg Inf A priori 1σ of axis tilts and turns, deg.
    opts.max_shift_km Inf Largest distance of an estimated center from its start, km.
    opts.max_tilt_deg Inf Largest angle of an estimated axis from its start, deg.
    opts.max_turn_deg Inf Largest turn of an estimated ellipsoid about its axis from its start, deg.
    opts.geometry_tol 10⁻³ Converged when every geometry step is below this fraction of its step limit.

    sbtk.invert.options(Name=Value) changes any of them; the fields can also be set afterwards.

    Example#

    MATLAB script: Estimation with several settings
    run("/path/to/SBTK/sbtk_setup.m")
body = sbtk.body.from_mass("Didymos", sbtk.shape.bundled("didymos"), 5.273e11, 0.43);
unc  = sbtk.data.uncertainties([sbtk.units.offset_to_degree1(0.002, 0.43), 1e-4, 2e-4, 4e-4], 2e-3, 0.02);
cof = body.centroid_km;                                                % positions from the center of figure
true_components = [sbtk.interior.sphere("core", cof + [0.03 -0.02 0.01], 0.17, +500), ...
                   sbtk.interior.torus("torus", 0.37, 0.055, 0.015, -500), ...
                   sbtk.interior.layer("regolith", 0, 0.012, -900)];
true_components(2).center_km = cof;                                    % the torus about the z-axis through cof
[exact, truth] = sbtk.data.simulate(body, sbtk.interior.create(true_components), 4, "estimation");
obs = sbtk.data.add_noise(sbtk.data.set_uncertainties(exact, unc), 1);

components = [sbtk.interior.sphere("core", cof, 0.17, 0), ...         % geometry fixed, densities estimated
              sbtk.interior.torus("torus", 0.37, 0.055, 0, 0), ...
              sbtk.interior.layer("regolith", 0, 0.012, 0)];
components(2).center_km = cof;
prob = sbtk.invert.setup(body, components, obs, sbtk.invert.options());
hom  = sbtk.invert.homogeneous(prob);                                  % is structure required?
fit  = sbtk.invert.run(prob);
sbtk.invert.summary(fit)

moving = components;  moving(1).estimate_position = true;              % estimate the core center as well
fit_p  = sbtk.invert.solve(body, moving, obs);
fprintf("core center [%.0f %.0f %.0f] m from the center of figure +- [%.0f %.0f %.0f] m\n", ...
        1e3*(fit_p.components(1).center_km - cof), 1e3*fit_p.center_sigma_km(1, :))

raised = components;  raised(2).estimate_position = [false false true];  % estimate the height of the torus
fit_z  = sbtk.invert.solve(body, raised, obs, sbtk.invert.options(max_shift_km=0.030));
fprintf("torus height %.1f m +- %.1f m (true 15 m)\n", 1e3*(fit_z.components(2).center_km(3) - cof(3)), 1e3*fit_z.center_sigma_km(2, 3))

    The true core is at (30, −20, 10) m from the center of figure and the true torus 15 m above it; the fixed-geometry fit places both at the center of figure. More examples: Calculations.
