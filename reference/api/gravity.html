    sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame#

    A gravity field about its center of mass, in the shape-model, spin or principal axes.

    Syntax

    MATLAB
    cf = sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame(set, body, axes)

    Arguments

    NameSizeClassUnitDefaultDescription
    set1 × 1structrequiredthe field: a measurement set (sbtk.data.simulate, sbtk.exchange.read_data, sbtk.exchange.read_shadr, ...), a true model (truth from sbtk.data.simulate) or an inversion result (fit from sbtk.invert.run: the field of the fitted model)
    body1 × 1structrequiredthe body: the shape model for a fitted model, the reference radius for a true model or a fitted model Fields: body.
    axes1 × 1stringrequired"shape": the axes of the shape-model frame; "spin": z along the axis of the largest moment of the field (the spin axis of a body in principal-axis rotation), x the shape-model x-axis projected onto the plane normal to it, y = z x x: the frame of body-fixed radio-science fields such as the OSIRIS-REx field of Bennu, with C21 = S21 = 0 and S22 not 0; "principal": the principal axes of inertia of the field (x the axis of the smallest moment, z of the largest, each pointing to the positive side of the shape-model axis nearest to it, right-handed), with C21 = S21 = S22 = 0 Validation: mustBeMember(axes, ["shape", "spin", "principal"]).

    Outputs

    NameDescription
    cfThe field about its center of mass in the chosen axes, with its principal axes and moments. Fields: cf.

    Description

    cf.labels1 x n names of the coefficients, 'C10' ... (degree 1 to cf.degree)
    cf.coef1 x n fully normalized coefficients about the center of mass: degree 1 is 0; in spin axes C21 = S21 = 0, in principal axes C21 = S21 = S22 = 0
    cf.sigma1 x n 1-sigma (NaN without uncertainties); degree 1: of the center of mass
    cf.covn x n covariance (NaN without uncertainties)
    cf.com_kmthe center of mass in the shape-model frame [km]
    cf.axes3 x 3, columns: the axes of cf.coef as unit vectors in the shape-model axes (eye(3) for "shape"); sbtk.body.to_frame, sbtk.data.to_frame and sbtk.interior.to_frame take cf.com_km and cf.axes to express everything in the frame with the center of mass as origin and these axes
    cf.principal3 x 3, columns: the principal axes (smallest, middle, largest moment) in the shape-model axes
    cf.moments1 x 3 principal moments minus their mean, A <= B <= C, as I / (M R_ref^2) (the degree-2 field fixes the moments up to their trace)

    cf.R_ref_km, cf.degree

    Moving the expansion point to the center of mass makes degree 1 vanish: about a point O, C10, C11, S11 = (z, x, y of the center of mass - O)/(sqrt(3) R_ref). The translation of solid harmonics mixes lower degrees into higher ones (degree 2 into 3, ...), so all coefficients change; it is exact (sbtk.internal.sh_translation). Rotating the axes mixes the 2l+1 coefficients within each degree and leaves every degree's power unchanged; in the principal axes the degree-2 inertia tensor is diagonal, so C21 = S21 = S22 = 0 and C20 = (A + B - 2C)/(2 M R^2), C22 = (B - A)/(4 M R^2) (unnormalized). The rotation is exact too (sbtk.internal.sh_rotation). In the spin axes only the tilt of the axis of the largest moment is removed: C21 = S21 = 0, and the angle of the axis of the smallest moment from x is (1/2) atan2(S22, C22).

    The center of mass: a true model's com_km; a fitted model's own center of mass (computed from its densities); for a measurement set, origin_km plus the offset its degree-1 coefficients give. A measurement set with coef_about 'origin' whose degree-1 coefficients put the center of mass more than 3 sigma from origin_km (more than 1 mm for exact values), e.g. a SHADR field about the shape-model origin, is moved to that center of mass; otherwise it is taken as about its center of mass already (its degree-1 values are then noise about zero). A set without degree-1 coefficients (e.g. sbtk.io.read_jpl_gravity) is taken as about its center of mass, origin_km; its degree-1 sigma is NaN. Moving a set and the principal axes need every coefficient of degree >= 2 up to the set's degree; a set that lacks some can be used only with axes "shape" and only if it is about its center of mass (missing coefficients are NaN).

    The covariance is propagated through the translation and the rotation as linear maps; the uncertainty of the center of mass and of the principal axes themselves, which the data also determine, is not added.

    Example

    MATLAB fragment from the help text. Uses variables defined by earlier calls: body, interior.
    [exact, truth] = sbtk.data.simulate(body, interior, 4, "T1");
cs = sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame(truth, body, "shape");       % degree 1 = 0
cz = sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame(truth, body, "spin");        % and C21 = S21 = 0
cp = sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame(truth, body, "principal");   % and C21 = S21 = S22 = 0
disp([cs.labels(1:8); string(cs.coef(1:8)); string(cp.coef(1:8))])
tilt_deg = acosd(cp.principal(3, 3))         % largest-moment axis from the shape z-axis

    sbtk.gravity.component_integrals#

    Integrals over one component per unit density, clipped to the body.

    Syntax

    MATLAB
    I = sbtk.gravity.component_integrals(geo, c)

    Arguments

    NameSizeClassUnitDefaultDescription
    geo1 × 1structrequiredGeometry from sbtk.gravity.geometry: the expansion origin and degree of the integrals. Fields: geo.
    c1 × 1structrequiredOne component. Fields: component.

    Outputs

    NameDescription
    IIntegrals of the part of the component inside the body, per unit density. Fields: integrals.

    Description

    I.U1 x (L+1)^2 int (r/R)^l Ybar_lm dV (packed as geo.pk) [km^3]
    I.Vvolume [km^3]
    I.m1first moment [km^4]
    I.Qsecond moments [xx yy zz xy xz yz] [km^5]

    all about geo.origin_km and limited to the body.

    A sphere entirely inside the body is an exact point mass. Every other component is integrated ray by ray: each interval of a sub-ray inside the component, between scaled facets s_a and s_b, adds its exact cone-scaling integrals (see sbtk.gravity.geometry). Layers and sectors give the interval directly; for spheres, ellipsoids and tori the crossings of each ray with their surface are the real roots of a quadratic (sphere, ellipsoid) or quartic (torus) in the distance along the ray, so a ray may cross a component any number of times and the integrals change smoothly with the component's geometry.

    Example

    MATLAB fragment from the help text. Uses variables defined by earlier calls: body.
    geo = sbtk.gravity.geometry(body, [0 0 0], 4);
I = sbtk.gravity.component_integrals(geo, sbtk.interior.sphere("core", [0 0 0], 0.170, 0));
fprintf('volume inside the body %.4f km^3\n', I.V);

    sbtk.gravity.forward#

    Forward model: gravity coefficients, center of mass and inertia of an interior.

    Syntax

    MATLAB
    fm = sbtk.gravity.forward(geo, interior, mass_kg)

    Arguments

    NameSizeClassUnitDefaultDescription
    geo1 × 1structrequiredGeometry from sbtk.gravity.geometry; the coefficients and inertia are about geo.origin_km. Fields: geo.
    interior1 × 1structrequiredComponents and random field. Fields: interior.
    mass_kgscalardoublekgrequiredTotal mass; sets the background density. Validation: mustBePositive.

    Outputs

    NameDescription
    fmBackground density, coefficients, center of mass and inertia. Fields: fm.

    Description

    density = background + sum of component relative densities + random field, with the background density set so that the total mass equals mass_kg.

    fm.rho_bg_kgm3background density [kg/m^3]
    fm.coef1 x (L+1)^2 fully normalized coefficients about geo.origin_km, packed as geo.pk (C00, C10, C11, S11, C20, ...)
    fm.com_kmcenter of mass in shape-model coordinates [km]
    fm.inertia_norm[Ixx Iyy Izz Ixy Ixz Iyz] about geo.origin_km / (M R_ref^2)
    fm.mass_kgmass_kg

    Example

    MATLAB fragment from the help text. Uses variables defined by earlier calls: body, interior.
    geo = sbtk.gravity.geometry(body, [0 0 0], 4);
fm  = sbtk.gravity.forward(geo, interior, body.mass_kg);

    sbtk.gravity.geometry#

    Facet-cone geometry of a body about an expansion origin (the integration workspace).

    Syntax

    MATLAB
    geo = sbtk.gravity.geometry(body, origin_km, degree)

    Arguments

    NameSizeClassUnitDefaultDescription
    body1 × 1structrequiredfrom sbtk.body.create Fields: body.
    origin_km1 × 3doublekmrequiredexpansion origin in shape-model coordinates [km] (1x3). Gravity coefficients, first moments and inertia are all about this point; layers and sectors are measured along rays from it. Validation: mustBeFinite.
    degreescalardoublerequiredhighest spherical-harmonic degree (0 when only volumes, inertia or densities are needed: much faster) Validation: mustBeInteger, mustBeNonnegative.

    Outputs

    NameDescription
    geoThe facet-cone geometry and integrals. Fields: geo.

    Description

    The body is the union of tetrahedral cones with apex at the origin and a surface facet as base. For every cone geo stores, per unit density,

    Tspherical-harmonic integrals int (r/R)^l Ybar_lm dV [km^3] (nf x (L+1)^2)
    Vvolume [km^3], m1 first moment [km^4],
    Qsecond moments [xx yy zz xy xz yz] [km^5]

    (T from the polyhedral kernel sbtk.internal.ComputePolyhedralCSBar_mex). Shrinking a cone about its apex by s multiplies r^l dV by s^(l+3), so the portion of a cone between the scaled facets s_a and s_b contributes exactly T (s_b^(l+3) - s_a^(l+3)), V (s_b^3 - s_a^3), m1 (s_b^4 - s_a^4), Q (s_b^5 - s_a^5). Interior components are described by such radial intervals along 9 sub-rays of every cone (sbtk.gravity.component_integrals).

    geo fields (km units throughout)

      body, origin_km, degree, R_ref_km, pk (coefficient packing, sbtk.internal.sh_packing)
  nf, K                 number of facets, sub-rays per facet (9)
  faces, verts          mesh with vertices relative to origin_km
  D, E, F, det6, EF, FD, DE    facet corners, 6 x cone volumes, edge cross products
  T, V, m1, Q           per-cone integrals (above)
  U_body, V_body, m1_body, Q_body   whole-body sums
  ray_t, ray_dir        sub-ray surface distances [km] and unit directions (nf*K rows)

    Didymos (49,152 facets): about 1 s at degree 4.

    Errors when some facet faces the origin (shape not star-shaped about it).

    Example

    MATLAB fragment from the help text. Uses variables defined by earlier calls: body.
    geo = sbtk.gravity.geometry(body, body.centroid_km, 4);
fprintf('%d facets, volume %.4f km^3\n', geo.nf, geo.V_body);

    sbtk.gravity.random_field_integrals#

    Density-weighted integrals of the random density field.

    Syntax

    MATLAB
    I = sbtk.gravity.random_field_integrals(geo, field)

    Arguments

    NameSizeClassUnitDefaultDescription
    geo1 × 1structrequiredGeometry from sbtk.gravity.geometry. Fields: geo.
    field1 × 1structrequiredThe random field. Fields: random_field.

    Outputs

    NameDescription
    IDensity-weighted integrals of the random field. Fields: integrals.

    Description

    I.U = int drho (r/R)^l Ybar_lm dV, I.V = int drho dV, I.m1, I.Q likewise (units: kg/m^3 times km^n). The field is evaluated at the centroid of every radial shell of every facet cone (sbtk.internal.body_samples) and each shell contributes its exact cone-scaling integrals with that value. No field (sigma_kgm3 = 0) gives zeros without sampling.

    Example

    MATLAB fragment from the help text. Uses variables defined by earlier calls: body.
    geo = sbtk.gravity.geometry(body, [0 0 0], 4);
I = sbtk.gravity.random_field_integrals(geo, sbtk.interior.random_field(40, 0.06, 7));
