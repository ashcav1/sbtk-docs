    Assumptions and limitations#

    Last edited October 21, 2026

    This page lists the physical and numerical assumptions of SBTK 1.0.0 and the calculations it does not perform. Each item links to the page where the behavior is described.

    Physical model#

    • The body is rigid and its density does not change with time. The gravity field is described by spherical-harmonic coefficients only; SBTK does not evaluate the potential or the gravitational acceleration at field points.
    • The density is a background density plus uniform components plus an optional Gaussian random field (Density distributions). Gradients within a component, other than through the random field, are not modeled.
    • The shape model is fixed and exact: its uncertainty is not propagated into the coefficients or the estimates.
    • The total mass is treated as exact. A measured GM enters only as the mass of the model; its uncertainty is not used (sbtk.exchange.read_shadr reads it and states that it is not used).

    Geometry#

    • The shape model must be closed, ordered outward and star-shaped about the expansion origin: every facet must face away from it, except small overhangs holding less than 10⁻⁶ of the volume (Shape models). Bodies with larger overhangs cannot be integrated.
    • Layers and sectors are defined along rays from the expansion origin (the center of mass), not along the local surface normal (Coordinate systems).
    • The z-axis of the shape-model frame is taken as the spin axis and as a principal axis in the definition of the libration ratio (Moments of inertia). For a homogeneous body the axis of the largest moment is 3.8° from the shape-model z-axis for Didymos and 0.7° for Dimorphos.
    • Measurements, models and estimates of one body are in one frame, that of its shape model. sbtk.data.to_frame turns the axes of a measurement set only when its inertia rows are exact (the values of sbtk.data.simulate): rows with uncertainties would mix with correlations that the rows cannot hold. An inversion result cannot be transformed; invert in the frame wanted (Frames of a gravity field).
    • The spin axes are those of a body in principal-axis rotation (z along the axis of the largest moment); the rotation state itself is not modeled, and a spin pole known with an error is represented only by the noise of C¯21, S¯21.
    • When a field with uncertainties is moved to its center of mass or rotated into its principal axes, the covariance is propagated through the translation and rotation as linear maps; the uncertainty of the center of mass and of the principal axes themselves is not added.

    Numerical integration#

    • Facet-cone integrals of the whole body are exact up to rounding (Gravity and moment calculations).
    • A sphere entirely inside the body is integrated as a point mass, which is exact.
    • Other components are integrated by sampling their radial extent along 9 sub-rays per facet and averaging the exact cone integrals over them. The result is exact when the radial interval is the same along every sub-ray of a cone and otherwise converges with the mesh resolution. Sector boundaries in latitude and longitude are resolved at the sub-ray spacing. The validation tests bound the errors for particular cases (Validation).
    • The crossings of a ray with a sphere, ellipsoid or torus are exact (roots of a quadratic or quartic). The integrals of these components are continuous in their position and orientation, but smooth only on scales larger than the ray spacing: where a ray grazes a component, its piece inside changes like a square root. The derivatives used to estimate geometry are therefore taken over steps of 0.1 m and 0.01° (Estimated geometry).
    • The random density field is integrated with one value per radial shell (24 shells per facet cone).

    Estimation#

    • The measurements are linear in the densities. Positions and axes of components, when estimated, are found by Gauss–Newton iteration with a trust region and optional a priori constraints and limits; the geometry of layers and sectors, and of every component not marked for estimation, is fixed. An estimated geometry can absorb signal from structure the model lacks (Estimated geometry).
    • The uncertainties are the linearized covariance with the mass constraint and fixed parameters eliminated. The inequality bounds on the densities are not reflected in the covariance; fit.at_bound flags densities at a bound.
    • The χ2 test assumes Gaussian measurement errors with the stated covariance.
    • Simulated noise is Gaussian, drawn from the stated covariance of the coefficients and from the σ of each inertia row independently (sbtk.data.add_noise).
    • The libration ratio obtained from a libration amplitude (sbtk.data.libration_ratio_from_amplitude) uses planar forced libration to first order in the eccentricity, about a point-mass primary; free libration, the primary's oblateness and the mutual dynamics of a binary are not included.

    Data formats#

    • Coefficients must be fully normalized without the Condon–Shortley phase (Gravity coefficients). sbtk.exchange.read_shadr rejects files whose header does not state full normalization; other conversions are the user's responsibility.
    • The exchange format gives the center of mass separately from the gravity coefficients, so a covariance between the center of mass and the other coefficients cannot be written (sbtk:exchange:correlatedCenterOfMass).
    • The SBTK measurement text format stores only the diagonal of the coefficient covariance; the .mat file keeps the full matrix (Results folders and figures).

    Software#

    • MATLAB R2024b or newer with the Optimization Toolbox (lsqlin). The compiled kernel is provided for macOS (Apple silicon and Intel) and must be compiled once on other platforms (Installation).
    • Version 1.0.0 is not publicly released.
