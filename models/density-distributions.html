    Density distributions#

    Last edited October 19, 2026

    Density model#

    The density at a point 𝐱 inside the body is

    ρ(𝐱)=ρbg+∑c:𝐱∈cΔρc+δρ(𝐱),

    where

    • ρbg is the background density, the density outside every component;
    • Δρc is the relative density of component c: its density minus the background density (component.drho_kgm3). Where components overlap their relative densities add. Everything outside the shape model is excluded;
    • δρ is an optional Gaussian random density field.

    The background density is not an input of a simulation: sbtk.gravity.forward sets it so that the total mass equals the mass of the body,

    ρbg=M−∑cΔρcVc−∫δρdVV,

    with Vc the volume of component c inside the body. In an inversion it is estimated or held at the bulk density (Density estimation). sbtk.data.simulate stops with sbtk:data:negativeDensity if the density is negative anywhere.

    An interior model (interior) holds the components and the random field; sbtk.interior.create builds it. sbtk.interior.density_at evaluates the density of any model at given points.

    Component types#

    Each constructor returns one component. A point 𝐱 (shape-model frame) belongs to the component when the condition in the table holds. 𝐨 is the expansion origin, ts the distance from 𝐨 to the surface along the ray through 𝐱, r=|𝐱−𝐨|.

    Type Constructor Membership condition Measured from
    Sphere sbtk.interior.sphere(name, center_km, radius_km, drho_kgm3) |𝐱−𝐜|<rs shape-model origin
    Ellipsoid sbtk.interior.ellipsoid(name, center_km, semi_axes_km, angles_deg, drho_kgm3) ∑i([R𝖳(𝐱−𝐜)]i/ai)2<1 shape-model origin
    Torus sbtk.interior.torus(name, ring_radius_km, tube_radius_km, height_km, drho_kgm3) (q12+q22−Rr)2+(q3−h)2<rt2 with q=A𝖳(x−c) its axis: the third axis of component.axes A through component.center_km c (the shape-model z-axis unless set)
    Layer sbtk.interior.layer(name, top_depth_km, bottom_depth_km, drho_kgm3) dtop≤ts−r<dbottom rays from 𝐨
    Sector sbtk.interior.sector(name, frac, lat_deg, lon_deg, drho_kgm3) fin≤r/ts<fout, ϕ1≤ϕ<ϕ2 (≤90° if ϕ2=90°), λ in [λ1,λ2) modulo 360° rays from 𝐨

    Notes on the types:

    • Sphere. A sphere entirely inside the body is integrated exactly as a point mass; one that crosses the surface is clipped. Its center can be estimated in an inversion (component.estimate_position).
    • Ellipsoid. R=Rz(yaw)Ry(pitch)Rx(roll) (Coordinate systems).
    • Torus. Lies about its axis, the shape-model z-axis through the origin as constructed; set component.center_km to move it (for example to the center of figure) and turn it with sbtk.interior.set_axis. It may be buried, cross the surface or extend outside the body. Only the part inside the body counts.
    • In another frame. sbtk.interior.to_frame moves and turns every component with the frame: centers, the axes of ellipsoids, the axis of a torus and the axes in which a sector's latitude and longitude are measured (component.axes), and the random field. The density at every point is unchanged (Frames of a gravity field).
    • Layer. Depth is measured along the ray from the expansion origin, not along the surface normal; layer("regolith", 0, 0.012, …) is the outermost 12 m along each ray.
    • Sector. Radial fractions follow the shape: sector("shell", [0.5 0.75], [-90 90], [-180 180], …) is a shell between half and three quarters of the way to the surface. sbtk.interior.sector stops with sbtk:interior:sectorRange unless each range increases and the longitude range spans at most 360°.

    sbtk.interior.latlon converts latitude, longitude and distance to a position, for example to place a sphere. sbtk.interior.summary prints a list of components.

    Where to place components#

    Spheres, ellipsoids and tori are placed in the coordinates of the shape-model file, so a center [0 0 0] is the origin of the file. Layers and sectors are measured along rays from the expansion origin, which in a simulation is the center of mass. The center of figure is the centroid of the shape model (body.centroid_km), the center of mass of a homogeneous body (Coordinate systems).

    Place components from the center of figure:

    • It depends only on the shape. The center of mass of a model depends on its components, so positions measured from it would depend on what they define.
    • "Central" means concentric with the figure. An offset of a component from the center of figure is what moves the center of mass away from it, which the data measure (degree 1). That offset should be chosen, not inherited from where the file has its origin.
    • The origin of a shape-model file can be far from the body's center. It is 57 m from the center of figure in the DART Didymos model v003 (13% of its radius), 0.7 m in the DART Dimorphos model v004, and 1.4 m in the OSIRIS-REx Bennu model, whose origin is the center of mass found from the tracking data.
    MATLAB fragment. Requires the variables defined in An interior model of Didymos.
    cof = body.centroid_km;                                  % center of figure
core = sbtk.interior.sphere("core", cof + [0.010 0 0], 0.170, +450);   % 10 m from the center of figure along x
t = sbtk.interior.torus("torus", 0.370, 0.055, 0, -500);
t.center_km = cof;                                       % about the z-axis through the center of figure

    In the interior of Cavale et al. (Icarus) for Bennu (Bennu) every position is from the origin of the Bennu shape model, which is its measured center of mass: there the two choices differ by 1.4 m. With a pre-mission model such as the DART Didymos v003 they differ by 57 m. A central core placed at the origin is then 57 m off center, and in a Didymos model it moves the center of mass about 2 m from the center of figure.

    Axes of components#

    The axis of a torus, the axes of an ellipsoid and the axes in which a sector's latitude and longitude are measured are component.axes; their third axis is "the axis" of the component.

    • sbtk.interior.set_axis(c, direction) turns a component so that its axis points along a direction (the smallest rotation, about its center).
    • sbtk.interior.align_with_spin_axis(body, interior, names, tol_deg, max_iter) sets the axis of the named components along the axis of the largest moment of the whole model: the spin axis of a body in principal-axis rotation, about which an equatorial torus forms. That axis depends on the components themselves, so the function iterates (simulate to degree 2, take the axis, turn the components, repeat) until the axis moves less than tol_deg, at most max_iter times (sbtk.internal.fixed_point, sbtk:iteration:notConverged). Other components are not changed.
    MATLAB fragment. Requires the variables defined in An interior model of Didymos.
    cof = body.centroid_km;
t = sbtk.interior.torus("torus", 0.370, 0.055, 0, -500);
t.center_km = cof;
model = sbtk.interior.create([sbtk.interior.sphere("boulder", cof + sbtk.interior.latlon(25, 60, 0.250), 0.040, +900), t], ...
                             sbtk.interior.no_random_field());
[model, spin_axis, n] = sbtk.interior.align_with_spin_axis(body, model, "torus", 0.01, 20);
fprintf("torus axis %.2f deg from the shape-model z-axis after %d simulations\n", acosd(spin_axis(3)), n)

    For a true model these are modeling choices. In an inversion the geometry of a torus or an ellipsoid (center, height, axis) can be estimated instead, with bounds and priors (Density estimation).

    Sector grids#

    sbtk.interior.sector_grid(frac_edges, lat_edges_deg, lon_edges_deg) divides the whole body into sectors, one per radial shell, latitude band and longitude band, named s<shell><lat><lon> (s111 is the innermost shell, the southernmost band, the first longitude band). This is a density model without an assumed structure. Because the sectors fill the body, the background density and a common change of all sector densities are indistinguishable: an inversion must hold the background at the bulk density (background = "bulk") or use regularization (Density estimation).

    Random density field#

    sbtk.interior.random_field(sigma_kgm3, corr_length_km, seed) is a Gaussian random field with standard deviation σ and correlation exp(−|Δ𝐱|2/(2L2)), represented by 300 Fourier modes (random_field). It is defined at every point independently of the expansion origin and is the same for the same seed. sbtk.interior.no_random_field gives no field. A random field is used in simulations; inversions do not estimate one.

    Prescribed components#

    A component with component.estimate_density set to false keeps its relative density in an inversion, for example a surface layer of known density. The constructors set it to true.

    Example#

    MATLAB script: An interior model of Didymos
    run("/path/to/SBTK/sbtk_setup.m")
body = sbtk.body.from_mass("Didymos", sbtk.shape.bundled("didymos"), 5.273e11, 0.43);
cof = body.centroid_km;                                          % positions from the center of figure
torus = sbtk.interior.torus("torus", 0.370, 0.055, 0, -500);
torus.center_km = cof;                                           % about the z-axis through it
components = [ ...
    sbtk.interior.sphere("core", cof, 0.170, +450), ...
    torus, ...
    sbtk.interior.layer("regolith", 0, 0.012, -900), ...
    sbtk.interior.sphere("boulder", cof + sbtk.interior.latlon(25, 60, 0.250), 0.040, +900)];
interior = sbtk.interior.create(components, sbtk.interior.random_field(40, 0.06, 7));
sbtk.interior.summary(interior.components)

geo = sbtk.gravity.geometry(body, body.centroid_km, 0);          % degree 0: no coefficients needed
fm  = sbtk.gravity.forward(geo, interior, body.mass_kg);
fprintf("background density %.0f kg/m^3\n", fm.rho_bg_kgm3)
model = interior;
model.rho_bg_kgm3 = fm.rho_bg_kgm3;                               % background + components + field
rho = sbtk.interior.density_at(model, body.centroid_km + [0 0 0; 0.30 0 0], geo)

    Here the geometry is built about the center of figure, so the layer is measured from it; sbtk.data.simulate uses the center of mass instead and returns the model with its background density as truth. The last line evaluates the density at the center of figure and 300 m from it along x. Further examples: Density models by component type.
