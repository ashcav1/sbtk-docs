    Gravity and moment calculations#

    Last edited October 20, 2026

    The forward model computes, for a density model inside a shape model, the gravity coefficients (Gravity coefficients), the center of mass and the inertia tensor (Moments of inertia) about an expansion origin 𝐨.

    Facet cones#

    sbtk.gravity.geometry(body, origin_km, degree) divides the body into tetrahedral cones, one per surface facet, with apex at 𝐨 and the facet as base. This partition is exact when the shape is star-shaped about 𝐨. For every cone f it stores, per unit density (geo):

    Quantity Definition Field
    Spherical-harmonic integrals Tf,lm=∫f(r/Rref)lY¯lmdV, with Y¯lm=P¯lm(sinϕ){cos,sin}(mλ)/(2l+1) geo.T
    Volume Vf geo.V
    First moments ∫f𝐱dV geo.m1
    Second moments ∫f[x2,y2,z2,xy,xz,yz]dV geo.Q

    Tf,lm comes from the polyhedral kernel sbtk.internal.ComputePolyhedralCSBar_mex applied to the tetrahedron of each cone. With this definition a body of uniform density ρ has C¯lm=ρ∑fTf,lm/M (with the unit factor of Units).

    Scaling within a cone#

    Shrinking a cone about its apex by a factor s multiplies rldV by sl+3. The part of cone f between the scaled facets sa and sb (0≤sa<sb≤1) therefore contributes exactly

    Tf,lm(sbl+3−sal+3),Vf(sb3−sa3),𝐦1,f(sb4−sa4),Qf(sb5−sa5).

    The validation test testConeScaling checks this law against the kernel to a relative error below 10⁻¹³.

    Integrals of components#

    sbtk.gravity.component_integrals(geo, c) returns the integrals of the part of a component inside the body per unit density (integrals):

    • Sphere entirely inside the body: exact point-mass integrals.
    • Layer: along each of 9 sub-rays per facet, the radial interval between the two depths gives sa and sb directly.
    • Sector: the radial fractions give sa and sb; whether a sub-ray lies within the latitude and longitude range is tested at the middle of the interval.
    • Sphere crossing the surface, ellipsoid, torus: a sub-ray crosses the component's surface where a polynomial in the distance along it vanishes: a quadratic for a sphere or an ellipsoid, a quartic for a torus (solved as the eigenvalues of its companion matrix). Its real roots between the expansion origin and the body's surface split the sub-ray, and each piece whose midpoint is inside the component contributes sbp−sap, so a ray may enter and leave the component several times, and a ray that only grazes it contributes a correspondingly short piece.

    For each facet the contributions are averaged over its 9 sub-rays and multiplied by the cone's integrals. The result is exact when the radial interval is the same along every sub-ray of a cone, and converges with mesh resolution otherwise (Assumptions and limitations).

    sbtk.gravity.random_field_integrals evaluates the random field at the centroid of each of 24 radial shells of every cone (samples) and integrates each shell exactly with that value.

    Coefficients, center of mass and inertia#

    sbtk.gravity.forward(geo, interior, mass_kg) combines the integrals of the body (U, V, 𝐦1, Q), of the components (Uc, Vc, …) and of the random field (Uδ, …):

    U=ρbgUbody+∑cΔρcUc+Uδ,C¯lm,S¯lm=UlmM, 𝐱cm=𝐨+ρbg𝐦1,body+∑cΔρc𝐦1,c+𝐦1,δM,Ixx=Qyy+Qzz, Ixy=−Qxy, …

    with ρbg chosen so that the mass is M (Density distributions). It returns the coefficients to degree L, the center of mass and [Ixx,Iyy,Izz,Ixy,Ixz,Iyz]/(MRref2) (fm).

    Simulated measurements#

    sbtk.data.simulate(body, interior, degree, name) runs the forward model about the center of mass:

    1. geometry and forward model about the shape-model origin;
    2. geometry and forward model about the resulting center of mass, repeated until the center of mass and the expansion origin differ by less than 1 mm (layers and sectors depend on the origin);
    3. a check that the density is not negative at the sample points of sbtk.internal.body_samples (24 radial shells per cone), otherwise sbtk:data:negativeDensity;
    4. the exact coefficients of degree 1 to L, the six inertia elements and the libration row, with the ratio k=(Iyy−Ixx)2+4Ixy2/Izz of the principal moments, as a measurement set without uncertainties (obs), and the true model (truth).

    No measurement uncertainties enter the forward model. To make measurements of the true model, sbtk.data.set_uncertainties (or sbtk.data.use_uncertainties, sbtk.data.set_covariance, sbtk.data.set_moi_sigma) gives the exact values uncertainties and sbtk.data.add_noise draws one noisy realization (Measurement sets). Selecting which inertia quantities a data set contains: sbtk.data.select_moi.

    Computation time#

    Times measured by tools/benchmark.m on Didymos (49,152 facets, degree 4), MATLAB R2025a on an Apple-silicon laptop:

    Step Time
    Geometry, degree 4 0.22 s
    Simulation: 4 components and a random field, center-of-mass frame 3.6 s
    Inversion setup: 3 components 1.3 s
    Inversion run 0.25 s
    Comparison with the true model 0.53 s

    The geometry scales with the number of facets; a 3,145,728-facet model takes about a minute to read and integrate.

    Example#

    MATLAB script: Coefficients, center of mass and inertia of a model
    run("/path/to/SBTK/sbtk_setup.m")
body = sbtk.body.from_mass("Didymos", sbtk.shape.bundled("didymos"), 5.273e11, 0.43);
interior = sbtk.interior.create(sbtk.interior.sphere("core", [0 0 0], 0.170, +450));

geo = sbtk.gravity.geometry(body, body.centroid_km, 4);       % about the center of figure, to degree 4
fm  = sbtk.gravity.forward(geo, interior, body.mass_kg);
lab = sbtk.gravity.labels(4);
fprintf("C20 = %.5e, C22 = %.5e\n", fm.coef(strcmp(lab, "C20")), fm.coef(strcmp(lab, "C22")))
fprintf("center of mass - center of figure = [%.2f %.2f %.2f] m\n", 1e3*(fm.com_km - body.centroid_km))
fprintf("Izz / (M R_ref^2) = %.4f\n", fm.inertia_norm(3))

    The core is centered at the shape-model origin, 57 m from the center of figure, so the center of mass moves toward it and the degree-1 coefficients about the center of figure are not zero.
