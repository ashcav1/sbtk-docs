    ROB-DATA5: Dimorphos#

    Last edited October 27, 2026

    In short. The smallest anomaly of the six: degree 2 differs from constant density mainly in C¯22 (+27σ), two moments are 4 to 5σ lower, and the center of mass is not measurably off. A single dense sphere, +293 kg/m³ placed 27 m from the center toward +x, explains all of it with 4 parameters (reduced χ² 0.03), and it reproduces ROB's exact values to degree 10 better than any other solution: the hidden interior is close to one dense body on the +x side. The sector grids and point masses fit with 3 to 8 effective parameters but reproduce little beyond degree 2. At the GRASS surface point every solution is within 2%, as constant density nearly is: the anomaly is too small for one point to choose between them.

    1. What the measurements say#

    • Ixx and Izz 5σ and 4σ lower: mass closer to the x-axis and to the spin axis, that is, along x.
    • The center of mass: 0.31 m toward +x, below its 1σ (0.4–0.6 m): no measurable offset.

    3. The solutions compared#

    • The models with a core match every coefficient and moment within 0.5σ. The point masses and the sector grids match the coefficients within 1.6σ but leave all three moments 2 to 3σ off: smoothed as much as the measurements allow, they do not reach the moments.
    • The one sphere does it with the fewest parameters that fit closely: AIC 8.4, against 18 for a core with a torus and a layer, which add parameters the measurements do not need, and 31 to 35 for the smoothed solutions.

    4. The best solution: one dense sphere toward +x#

    Output
    component  type          center - cof (m)   dist m      lat      lon    drho kg/m^3        rho      mass kg  makes most of (sigma)
core       sphere     [   27    -0     7]       28       15       -0      293 +-  25       1813      3.3e+07  C22 +27, C20 +6, C11 +1

    • The sphere (+293 ± 25 kg/m³, 3.3 × 10⁷ kg above the background, 1.2% of the mass) on the x-axis makes C¯22 (+27σ) and the lower J2 (+6σ in C¯20) at once: a mass on the x-axis lengthens the body along x. Its offset of 27 m with this mass moves the center of mass by 0.33 m, within the measured 0.31 ± 0.4 m.

    The model of one sphere and the search's model are nearly the same (correlation 0.88): one dense body of about 30 m radius, 27 to 29 m toward +x; the search's torus and layer are nearly empty. The point masses and the sector grid move half as much mass or less (0.4–0.6% of M against 1.0–1.1%) and keep it near the center: the point masses in two lobes 10 to 15 m on either side of the center along x, the sector grid in the inner sectors, a little more toward +x (its excess centered 10 m toward +x). All four put the extra mass along the x-axis, which is what C¯22 says; whether it is one body off center or spread about the center, the measurements leave open (the center-of-mass offset is below its 1σ). ROB's exact values to degree 10 favor the one sphere (section 7).

The hidden interior is, to this precision, one dense compact body.
