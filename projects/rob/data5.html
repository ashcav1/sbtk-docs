    ROB-DATA5: Dimorphos#

    Last edited October 27, 2026

    Result. The simplest Dimorphos package: one sphere about 300 kg/m³ denser than the background, 27 m from the center of figure toward +x, explains the coefficients and the inertia (reduced χ2 0.03) and predicts the surface acceleration at the lander point to 0.2%. It also reproduces 38–99% of the anomaly of every degree up to 10 of the exact values, far beyond the degree 2 the radio-science precision resolves. The anomaly is small: only 5 of the 18 measurements differ from constant density by more than 1σ. The package's mass corresponds to 1539 kg/m³.

    • At the Hera radio-science precision for Dimorphos, degree 1 (the center of mass, to about 0.5 m) and degree 3 (to about 10⁻²) carry no information: the measurements are degree 2 and the three inertia elements.
    • Degree 2 is scaled by −0.7% (a small central condensation), and its rest is mostly C¯22: 27σ larger than the homogeneous value, the elongation of the body along its long axis enhanced.
    • The three moments of inertia are 3–5σ smaller than the homogeneous body's: mass slightly toward the center.
    • No compact mass converges from degrees 1 and 2 alone: the center-of-mass offset (0.3 m) is not distinguished from 0 at this precision.

best: core, placed
    • One sphere placed by its χ2 profile (+293 ± 25 kg/m³ at 27 m toward +x): reduced χ2 0.03, the model with the fewest parameters that is consistent. A sphere along the long axis raises C¯22 and moves the center of mass toward +x.
    • Adding a layer, a torus, or making the core an ellipsoid fits as well and does not change the sphere: the layer, torus and ellipsoid densities are all within 1–2σ of 0.
    • A core at the center of figure cannot (246): the mass excess must be off center along x.

    With only degree 2 resolved, the truncation test is short: the sphere fitted to degrees 1–2 predicts degree 3 within its (large) uncertainties. The exact values tell more: the same sphere reproduces 92% of degree 4, 97% of degree 6, and 87–99% of degrees 7–10, although it was fitted to degree 2 and the inertia only. A compact dense body in a homogeneous body is the whole story at every scale the coefficients see; if the package were measured to degree 10, the model would not have to be truncated.

    The surface point is near the +x end of the body, 86 m from the center. The homogeneous body predicts 0.6% too little acceleration there; every model with the sphere toward +x predicts it to within 0.2% and 0.13°, while an elongated core 16 m toward −x misses by 1.2% and the one-shell sector model by 2.0% and 2.1°. The gravimeter confirms the reading of the coefficients (Surface point scan shows what a buried body under the point would change).
