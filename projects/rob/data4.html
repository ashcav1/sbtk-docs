    ROB-DATA4: Dimorphos#

    Last edited October 30, 2026

    ROB-DATA4 is the package where the measurements barely decide and the truth does. Degree 2 and the moments of inertia say mass has moved toward the two ends of the y-axis, and degree 1 that the −y end is heavier. A dense core placed along the center-of-mass offset, with a torus and a surface layer, fits with 6 parameters and ranks first by AIC, 4.6 ahead of the sector grid and 5.4 ahead of the point masses. All three solutions put the excess on the −y side; they differ in how compact it is. ROB's exact values favor the compact core: it reproduces 81% of degrees 4 to 10, which no solution was fitted to, the sector grid 25%, and the point masses do worse than constant density. At the GRASS surface point above it the core predicts an acceleration 3.7% too large, the sector grid and the point masses 7 to 9% too small.

    1. What the measurements show#

    Measurement C − C_CD 1σ In σ What it says
    S¯11 −1.13×10−2 3.76×10−3 −3 center of mass toward −y
    C¯20 −1.77×10−3 8.57×10−5 −21 J2 higher: mass toward the equator
    S¯21 1.32×10−3 3.13×10−4 +4 mass toward +y in the north, −y in the south
    C¯22 −4.94×10−3 9.93×10−5 −50 mass toward ±y more than ±x
    S¯22 9.09×10−4 8.75×10−5 +10 mass toward longitudes 45° and −135°
    Ixx/MR2 7.62×10−3 5.74×10−4 +13 mass farther from the x-axis

    Izz/MR2 5.24×10−3 7.12×10−4 +7 mass farther from the z-axis

    The measurements more than 3σ from constant density.

    C¯22 is 50σ below its value at constant density: mass has moved toward the ends of the y-axis. The moments of inertia agree: Iyy is smaller (mass closer to the y-axis), Ixx and Izz larger. J2 is 21σ higher, mass toward the equator. The center of mass is 1.8 m toward −y, 3σ: the −y end carries more.

    2. Where the mass is#

    Degree 2 puts the excess at both ends of an axis through latitude −9°, longitude −95° and the opposite point: the y-axis, tilted 9°. The center of mass points to the same place, −8°, −95°. The model built for this package therefore places a dense core along the center-of-mass offset by its χ² profile and then fits its position by Gauss–Newton, with a torus and a surface layer for the part of J2 the core does not make.

    3. How well each solution fits#

    All three match every measurement within 1σ. The model with components needs 6 parameters, the sector grid and the point masses 8.0 and 8.6 effective parameters. The components rank first by 4.6 in AIC, a preference but not a strong one. With 18 measurements, degree 2 and the moments determine where the excess is but not its form.

    Background density 1436 kg/m³ (bulk density 1540 kg/m³). Makes most of: the component's part of each coefficient in units of its 1σ.

    The core alone makes C¯22 and S¯22 and the center-of-mass offset. It is a sphere of 30 m radius, 50 m from the center toward −y, 984 ± 514 kg/m³ denser than the background. The torus and the layer make the rest of J2, and neither is resolved. Degree 2 and the moments allow a denser core closer in or a lighter one farther out: from where its χ² profile placed it, Gauss–Newton moves the core outward and lowers its density, and the wide 1σ of its density says how loosely the two are held apart.

    Mass moved: the mass above the bulk density, as a percentage of the mass of the body. Excess and deficit centers are from the center of figure. Correlation: of the relative densities of the solutions, 8.3 m cell by 8.3 m cell.

    All three put the excess on the −y side: its center is 49 m toward −y for the components, 34 m for the sector grid and 15 m for the point masses. The point masses mimic the core. On the z = 0 plane they gather a dense lobe 10 to 50 m toward −y, where the core is, with light bands on either side, but they keep it closer to the center and spread it out. The sector grid spreads it over the whole −y sector of its outer shell. The coefficients cannot choose between these forms: the cell-by-cell correlation is 0.49 between the components and the sector grid, 0.34 between the sector grid and the point masses, and 0.15 between the components and the point masses.

    6. Against ROB's exact values and the GRASS point#

    Beyond degree 2 the three separate. Degree 3 is fitted, but its 1σ is so large that the fit does not constrain it: the core reproduces 97% of it, the sector grid 11%, and the point masses do worse than constant density. Over degrees 4 to 10, which no solution was fitted to, the core reproduces 81%: 94% of degree 4, 71% of degree 5, 22% of degree 6, and none of degrees 7 to 10. The sector grid reproduces 25% and the point masses do worse than constant density (−0.33). A compact core makes the low degrees the hidden interior has; a spread excess does not.

    Solution Acceleration minus ROB's (% of ROB's) Angle from ROB's
    Components +3.7% 1.5°
    Sector grid −7.3% 0.7°
    Point masses, sensitivity weighting −9.3% 0.8°
    Point masses, equal weighting −6.6% 0.3°
    Constant density −13.3% 0.9°

    The acceleration each solution predicts at the GRASS surface point minus ROB's, in magnitude (% of ROB's magnitude) and direction, on the full-resolution shape model.

    The GRASS point lies on the surface at the −y end, above the core. There the excess matters most: constant density predicts an acceleration 13.3% too small. The core predicts it 3.7% too large, 1.5° from ROB's direction; the sector grid and the point masses, whose excess is farther from the surface, 7.3% and 9.3% too small. One surface measurement over the anomaly separates solutions that the gravity coefficients cannot. Here the truth lies between them: the core puts too much mass close to the point, the spread solutions too little.
