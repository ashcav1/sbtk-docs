    ROB-DATA4: Dimorphos#

    Last edited October 27, 2026

    In short. Mass is moved from the ends of the long x-axis toward ±y: C¯22 changes sign (50σ) and the moments of inertia say the same (Ixx +13σ, Iyy −10σ). Dimorphos is measured to degree 2 and its three moments, 18 numbers, which several interiors fit. The two best by AIC are both a dense core 31 to 46 m toward −y with a torus and a dense layer (reduced χ² 0.08 and 0.65, 6 to 9 parameters); the sector grids and point masses fit with 4 to 5 effective parameters but not as closely. The GRASS surface point, which no solution was fitted to, decides: the core model predicts the lander's acceleration to 0.2%, the sector grids and point masses miss it by 10 to 11%.

    • C¯22 is 50σ below constant density: the anomaly takes away the homogeneous body's elongation along x; S¯22 +10σ turns what is left a little.
    • The moments: Ixx 13σ higher and Iyy 10σ lower than constant density: mass farther from the x-axis and closer to the y-axis, that is, moved from the ends of x toward ±y. Izz 7σ higher: mass farther from the spin axis.
    • J2 21σ higher: mass toward the equator.
    • The center of mass is 1.8 m toward −y, 3σ at this precision.

    3. The solutions compared#

    • The search's core model matches every coefficient and moment within 1σ. The model with components leaves 2.5σ in S¯11 and C¯22 and 1.6σ in J2.
    • The sector grids and the point masses, smoothed as much as the measurements allow, leave about 3σ in J2, C¯22 and S¯11: with 18 numbers, the smoothing that keeps them consistent also keeps them from the largest coefficients.

    4. The best solutions: a dense core toward −y, a torus and a dense layer#

    Why the components are where they are:

    • The dense core toward −y makes most of the change of C¯22 (−50σ in the search's model): mass on the y-axis lowers C¯22. Being on the −y side, it also makes the center-of-mass offset.
    • The torus and the layer add mass in the equator and near the surface: J2 higher and the moments larger. In the search's model they are not resolved (their 1σ exceeds their densities); in the model with components the torus is +419 ± 170 kg/m³ and the layer +1790 ± 800 kg/m³ (a crust about 2 m thick at about 2970 kg/m³ over a background of 1180).
    • The two core models differ in where they put the core: 31 m out at +2530 kg/m³, or 46 m out at +1165 kg/m³. The coefficients do not tell them apart; the surface point does (section 6).

    All four put more mass toward the ±y axis than toward ±x, which is what C¯22 and the moments say, but in different ways. The two core models put the excess in one dense core toward −y (centered 44 and 27 m toward −y), with a dense crust around a slightly lighter interior; they correlate 0.67. The sector grid makes the outer sectors at both ends of the y-axis denser and those toward ±x lighter; the point masses put the excess in a band along the y-axis through the center, with lighter lobes on both sides along x. Both smooth solutions are nearly symmetric (their excess is centered within 2 m of the center of figure); they correlate 0.40 with each other and 0.13 to 0.31 with the core models. Whether the extra mass is one body on the −y side or spread over both ends, the coefficients do not decide (the center-of-mass offset is 3σ); the GRASS surface point does (section 6).

    6. Independent check: the GRASS surface point#

    ROB's package gives the gravitational acceleration at one point on the surface, 81 m toward −y, where the GRASS gravimeter of the Juventas lander would measure it. No solution was fitted to it; each predicts it from its interior:

    • Constant density predicts 13% too little acceleration there: the extra mass toward −y is under the lander.
    • The model with components predicts the acceleration to −0.2% and its direction to 0.2°. The search's model, whose core is farther out and less dense, gives +2.0% and 1.4°.
    • The sector grids and the point masses spread the extra mass over the whole ±y side and give −10% to −11%.

    The coefficients cannot tell the core models from the smooth ones; a single surface measurement can.
