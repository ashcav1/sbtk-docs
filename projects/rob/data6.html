    ROB-DATA6: Dimorphos#

    Last edited October 27, 2026

    Result. Mass is pushed outward: degree 2 is 11% larger than the homogeneous body's and all three moments of inertia are 30 to 60σ larger. A light core 54 m from the center of figure toward −x, a dense equatorial torus and a dense surface layer explain the coefficients and the inertia (reduced χ2 0.39). Without a ring the dense outside cannot be fitted, and a model of sectors needs two shells. At the surface point near the +x end every consistent model predicts the acceleration's magnitude to within a few percent but its direction 2.6–3° off, as does the homogeneous body: the material near the lander is not resolved. The package's mass corresponds to 1594 kg/m³, the highest of the three Dimorphos packages.

best: core, placed; torus; layer
    • A light core alone goes 46 m toward −x and −z (−1700 kg/m³, almost empty): reduced χ2 660. A dense layer brings it to 254.
    • A light core, a dense torus about the z-axis and a dense layer: 0.39. The torus (+816 ± 66 kg/m³) carries the equatorial excess; the layer (+2214 ± 345 kg/m³: a crust about 2.3 m thick at about 3600 kg/m³) and the light core (−950 ± 370 kg/m³) the outward shift. With the torus height and axis free, the same within 1σ.
    • Ellipsoidal cores without a torus: 52 to 205. Sectors of one shell cannot fit (1650); of two shells they reach 1.86 with the largest consistent regularization, a lighter inner shell and a denser outer one.

    The model reproduces all of degree 2 and half of degree 1 (the center of mass, measured only to 0.5 m), and none of degrees 3–10 of the exact values: the anomaly at those scales has another shape than a torus and a crust. At the radio-science precision this cannot be seen, since only degree 2 is resolved; with better data the model would have to be truncated at degree 2.
