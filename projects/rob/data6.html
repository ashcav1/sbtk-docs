    ROB-DATA6: Dimorphos#

    Last edited October 27, 2026

    In short. Mass is pushed outward and toward ±y: the moments of inertia are 30 to 60σ larger than constant density's, J2 is 79σ higher, and C¯22 changes sign (−97σ). The best solution is a light core 54 m toward −x (−950 kg/m³), a dense torus about the spin axis (+820 kg/m³) and a dense crust (+2210 kg/m³): reduced χ² 0.39 with 6 parameters, far ahead of the smoothed solutions by AIC (17 against 33 to 35). It fits degree 2 and the moments, which is all Dimorphos is measured to; beyond, ROB's exact values show structure that none of the solutions has. At the GRASS surface point the smooth solutions predict the size of the acceleration better (0.1% to 1.6% off against 4% for the core model), and every solution, like constant density, is 2.5 to 3.7° off in direction: the material near the lander is not determined by the coefficients.

    • The center of mass: 1.3 m toward +x, 3σ at this precision.

    • Degree 2 moves mass toward the ±y axis (longitudes −85° and 95°, ±1°) and away from an axis close to the poles (latitude ±71°): toward the equator and toward ±y.

    So the model given to the fit was a core placed by its χ² profile along the offset (it went 54 m toward −x and became light), a torus about the z-axis for the equatorial excess, and a surface layer for the outward shift.

    3. The solutions compared#

    • The model with components matches every coefficient and moment within 2σ (its largest misfits C¯11 and C¯22, 1.8σ).
    • The point masses and the sector grids leave the moments 2 to 2.6σ too small: smoothed as much as the measurements allow, they do not push enough mass outward. The search's model misses the offset by 2.9σ in C¯10.

    4. The best solution: a light core, a dense torus and a dense crust#

    Output
    component  type          center - cof (m)   dist m      lat      lon    drho kg/m^3        rho      mass kg  makes most of (sigma)
core       sphere     [  -54    -5     8]       54        8     -175     -953 +- 371        430    -1.07e+08  C22 -93, C20 +16, S22 -11

    Why each component is where it is, and dense or light:

    • The light core (−950 ± 370 kg/m³, 54 m toward −x, 430 kg/m³ in all) makes nearly all of the change of C¯22 (−93σ): mass missing on the x-axis is the same, in degree 2, as mass added along y. Being on the −x side, it moves the center of mass toward +x.
    • The dense torus (+816 ± 66 kg/m³) puts mass in the equatorial plane: J2 higher (−68σ in C¯20).
    • The dense crust (+2210 ± 350 kg/m³, about 2 m thick: 3600 kg/m³) adds to J2 (−27σ) and, with the torus, carries the larger moments: mass outward.

    All four make the interior lighter and the outer part denser, and move more mass than in any other package (5.9% to 10.8% of M): what the larger moments say. The model with components and the search's model are alike (correlation 0.62): a light core toward −x (their deficit centered 13 and 14 m toward −x), a dense ring in the equatorial plane around it and a dense crust. The point masses and the sector grid (correlation 0.47 with each other, 0.12 to 0.37 with the models with components) keep the light part at the center of figure, the point masses as a narrow column along the spin axis, the sector grid as its inner sectors, and make the outer part denser toward ±y, which C¯22 asks for. Light inside and dense outside is common to all; that the light part is off center toward −x, as the 3σ center-of-mass offset suggests, comes from the models with components only.

    The surface point is 86 m toward +x. Constant density predicts 1.8% too little acceleration there and 2.6° off in direction. The smoothed solutions predict the size best (point masses with equal weighting +0.05%, sector grid +0.9%); the models with a light core and a dense crust give +3.9% to +6.7%: their crust is too dense near this point. Every solution stays 2.5° to 3.7° off in direction, as constant density is (2.6°): the coefficients and the moments do not constrain the material near the lander, and on DATA6 the surface point does not confirm the best model by AIC.
