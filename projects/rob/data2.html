    ROB-DATA2: Didymos#

    Last edited October 30, 2026

    ROB-DATA2 is the package where simple shapes fail. The center of mass is 2.3 m off center toward the south, degree 2 is tilted 58° from the equator, and degree 3, small in the other Didymos packages, is large. No model built from a core, a torus and a layer comes near the measurements. A dense and a light sphere started where the point masses put the mass, with four compact masses added where the measurements ask for them, does fit, and ties in AIC with a grid of 32 sectors and with the point masses. The sector grid and the point masses agree closely, on a dense region below the equator toward −x and −y and a light region above it toward +y: their excess centers are 10 m apart and their relative densities correlate 0.78. Above the fitted degrees, 1 to 5, the point masses reproduce 17% of ROB's exact values and the sector grid 5%; the model with components does worse than constant density.

    The measurements more than 3σ from constant density.

    The center of mass is 2.3 m from the center of figure, four times as far as in ROB-DATA1 and ROB-DATA3: toward −y (171σ), −x (58σ) and the south (17σ). J2 is 53σ lower than at constant density: mass has moved toward the poles or the center. C¯21 and S¯21 are not zero, so the anomaly is not symmetric about the equator, and every order of degree 3 is measured. Degree 5 is resolved too: S¯52 and S¯53 are 5σ and 4σ from constant density.

    2. Where the mass is#

    Degree 2 puts the excess at latitude −58°, longitude −176°, and at the opposite point: an axis tilted 58° from the equator. The center of mass points to −45°, −108°, the southern side. Degree 3 has its excess at −36°, 162° and its deficit at the opposite point, and degree 5 its excess at −14°, 152°. Degrees 1, 2, 3 and 5 all point to the southern hemisphere, between longitudes 150° and −110° across 180°.

    3. How well each solution fits#

    The three match every fitted coefficient within 3σ: the point masses within about 1σ, the model with components within 2σ, the sector grid within 3σ. The sector grid needs 25 effective parameters, the model with components 24 and the point masses 30. Their AIC spans 1.3, a tie. The models of a core, a torus and a layer do not fit: a core placed along the center-of-mass offset with a tilted torus and a layer has a reduced χ² of 20, and with three compact masses added, 16. A core can move the center of mass and a torus can change J2, but neither makes a tilted elongation together with a large degree 3.

    Background density 2527 kg/m³ (bulk density 2606 kg/m³). Makes most of: the component's part of each coefficient in units of its 1σ.

    The model starts from the point masses. A dense and a light sphere, each of 0.3 mean radii, are placed at the centroids of the point masses' excess and deficit, and their densities and centers are fitted. Compact masses, spheres of 91 m radius, are then added one at a time where the residuals ask for them, each kept while it passes the likelihood-ratio test at the 5% level, and every position is refitted each time. Four pass.

    • The dense sphere (+1893 ± 213 kg/m³, radius 110 m, 145 m from the center toward latitude −33°, longitude 180°) makes most of the center-of-mass offset toward −x and the south, and of the change of J2.
    • The light sphere (−234 ± 112 kg/m³, 250 m from the center toward latitude 25°, longitude 157°) is the deficit above the equator.
    • The four compact masses (+207 to +1093 kg/m³, 172 to 295 m from the center, three of them near the equator toward longitudes −66° to −107° and 13°) move the center of mass toward −y and shape degrees 3 to 5. Two are not resolved: the fourth (+207 ± 267 kg/m³) and the one toward −107° (+284 ± 253 kg/m³).

    Together the six spheres move 2.8% of the mass of the body, three times as much as the sector grid or the point masses.

    5. Point masses, sector grid and components side by side#

    Each of the 32 sectors reaches from the center to the surface. The densest are south of −45° latitude: +299 ± 54 kg/m³ toward longitudes −180° to −135°, +170 ± 53 toward 135° to 180° and +133 ± 67 toward 45° to 90°. The lightest is between 0° and 45° latitude toward longitudes 135° to 180° (−106 ± 10 kg/m³). The sectors north of 45° are not resolved.

    The sector grid and the point masses are built differently: 32 large blocks against 2,667 point masses, with different regularization. They still place the anomaly in the same way. Each moves about 1% of the mass of the body. Each centers its excess about 140 m from the center toward latitude −49°, longitude −135°, within 10 m of the other, and its deficit about 115 m toward +y and the north. On the y = 0 plane the point masses gather the excess into a lobe 100 to 250 m below the center, where the sector grid has its densest southern sectors. The model with components puts its excess in the same direction but nearer the center, 67 m from it, and its deficit 35 m from it; it correlates 0.29 and 0.36 with the other two.

    6. Against ROB's exact values#

    All three reproduce degrees 1 to 3. Of degrees 4 and 5 the point masses reproduce 99% and 88%, the model with components 94% and 86%, the sector grid 83% and 78%. Above degree 5, which none was fitted to, the point masses and the sector grid fall toward zero: 22 to 26% of degree 6, less of each degree after it. The model with components does worse than constant density at every degree above 5. Over degrees 6 to 10 the point masses reproduce 17%, the sector grid 5%, and the model with components −0.20: its compact masses fit degrees 1 to 5 with structure the hidden interior does not have. The large-scale structure the sector grid and the point masses agree on is real; its finer form is beyond what degrees 1 to 5 can show.
