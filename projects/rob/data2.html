    ROB-DATA2: Didymos#

    Last edited November 3, 2026

    ROB-DATA2 is the package where simple shapes fail. The center of mass is 2.3 m off center toward the south, degree 2 is tilted 58° from the equator, and degree 3, small in the other Didymos packages, is large. No model built from a core, a torus and a layer comes near the measurements. The sectors and the point masses agree closely, on a dense region below the equator toward −x and −y and a light region above it toward +y: the mass above the bulk density is centered within 10 m in the two, and their relative densities correlate 0.78. Three large shapes make that with 20 parameters: a dense body to the south, a dense tilted lens below the equator and a dense crust with a gap. This model ranks first by AIC, 2.3 ahead of the sectors and 3.7 ahead of the point masses. Its shapes came from a search of 17,400, which AIC does not charge for, so the lead is not decisive. Above the fitted degrees, 1 to 5, the point masses reproduce 17% of ROB's exact values, the model with components 9% and the sectors 5%.

    The measurements more than 3σ from constant density.

    The center of mass is 2.3 m from the center of figure, four times as far as in ROB-DATA1 and ROB-DATA3: toward −y (171σ), −x (58σ) and the south (17σ). J2 is 53σ lower than at constant density: mass has moved toward the poles or the center. C¯21 and S¯21 are not zero, so the anomaly is not symmetric about the equator, and every order of degree 3 is measured. Degree 5 is resolved too: S¯52 and S¯53 are 5σ and 4σ from constant density.

    2. Where the mass is#

    Degree 2 puts the excess at latitude −58°, longitude −176°, and at the opposite point: an axis tilted 58° from the equator. The center of mass points to −45°, −108°, the southern side. Degree 3 has its excess at −36°, 162° and its deficit at the opposite point, and degree 5 its excess at −14°, 152°. Degrees 1, 2, 3 and 5 all point to the southern hemisphere, between longitudes 150° and −110° across 180°.

    3. How well each solution fits#

    The three match every fitted coefficient within 3σ: the point masses within about 1σ, the model with components within 2σ, the sectors within 3σ. The model with components needs 20 parameters, the sectors 25 effective parameters and the point masses 30. The models of a core, a torus and a layer do not fit: a core placed along the center-of-mass offset with a tilted torus and a layer has a reduced χ² of 20, and with three compact masses added, 16. A core can move the center of mass and a torus can change J2, but neither makes a tilted elongation together with a large degree 3.

    Background density 2440 kg/m³ (bulk density 2606 kg/m³). Makes most of: the component's part of each coefficient in units of its 1σ.

    The sectors and the point masses show a dense south and a light north, not compact masses. This model makes that from a few large shapes, found by fitting pairs and triples of 17,400 large ellipsoids and sectors and refining the best. Eight of its 20 parameters are sizes and the crust's geometry, chosen in that search.

    • The south body (+115 ± 7 kg/m³, a sphere of radius 283 m that the surface clips, 155 m from the center toward latitude −11°, longitude −92°) makes most of the center-of-mass offset toward −y.
    • The lens (+175 ± 7 kg/m³, semi-axes of 230, 486 and 175 m, tilted, 163 m from the center toward latitude −51°, longitude 157°) lowers J2, as the measurements ask.
    • The crust (+462 ± 54 kg/m³, the outer 5% of the way to the surface) raises the moments, and its gap of 13° about latitude 27°, longitude 151° moves the center of mass away from the gap.

    The background is 2440 ± 10 kg/m³ against a bulk density of 2606 kg/m³, and where the south body and the lens overlap their densities add, to 2730 kg/m³. Each shape holds about 2% of the mass of the body. This is one model that fits, not the only one: it correlates only 0.42 with the sectors and with the point masses.

    5. Point masses, sectors and components side by side#

    Each of the 32 sectors reaches from the center to the surface. The densest are south of −45° latitude: +299 ± 54 kg/m³ toward longitudes −180° to −135°, +170 ± 53 toward 135° to 180° and +133 ± 67 toward 45° to 90°. The lightest is between 0° and 45° latitude toward longitudes 135° to 180° (−106 ± 10 kg/m³). The sectors north of 45° are not resolved.

    The sectors and the point masses are built differently: 32 large blocks against 2,667 point masses, with different regularization. They still place the anomaly in the same way. Each moves about 1% of the mass of the body. Each centers the mass above the bulk density about 140 m from the center toward latitude −49°, longitude −135°, within 10 m of the other, and the mass below it about 115 m toward +y and the north. On the y = 0 plane the point masses gather the dense region into a lobe 100 to 250 m below the center, where the sectors have their densest southern blocks. The model with components makes the same directions nearer the center, 67 m and 70 m from it, and moves 1.2% of the mass.

    6. Against ROB's exact values#

    All three reproduce degrees 1 to 3. Of degrees 4 and 5 the point masses reproduce 99% and 88%, the sectors 83% and 78%, the model with components 93% and 42%. Above degree 5, which none was fitted to, all three fall toward zero: 22 to 26% of degree 6, and at most 30% of any degree after it. Over degrees 6 to 10 the point masses reproduce 17%, the model with components 9% and the sectors 5%. The large-scale structure all three share is what the measurements fix; the fitted degrees do not fix its smaller scale.
