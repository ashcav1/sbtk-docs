    ROB-DATA3: Didymos#

    Last edited October 27, 2026

    In short. The even zonal coefficients change far more than anything else: J2 is 570σ lower and J4 95σ higher than constant density, the signature of a ring about the spin axis. The best solution is a dense core at the center (+1200 kg/m³), a light, thick torus about the z-axis (−425 kg/m³, ring radius 383 m, tube radius 110 m) and a dense thin layer: reduced χ² 1.22 (p = 0.18) with 6 parameters, and it reproduces ROB's exact values to degree 7. The point masses come close but need 30 to 32 of the 38 measurements' degrees of freedom; the sector grids, whose sectors run from the center to the surface, cannot make a ring at all.

    • C¯20 +570σ (J2 lower) and C¯40 −95σ (J4 higher): the zonal terms of degrees 2 and 4 change by 12% and 35% of their constant-density values; degrees 3 and 5 by a few percent. Only the even degrees changing is what a ring or a torus about the spin axis does, and J2 lower means less mass in the equatorial plane: the ring is light.
    • The center of mass is 0.56 m off (toward −x and +y): something off center, but small.
    • C¯22, S¯22, S¯42 (9σ, 17σ, 15σ): the body's elongation changes too.

    3. The solutions compared#

    • The model with components and the search keep J2 and J4, the two largest coefficients by far, within 1σ; their largest misfits are small terms of degrees 2 to 4 (C¯32, C¯43, S¯22, S¯43, 2.0 to 2.4σ).
    • The point masses match every coefficient within 1σ except J4 (3.7σ too high), using nearly all the freedom the measurements have (30 of 38 effective parameters).
    • The sector grids fail: a sector spans the full depth (or half of it) and cannot put a light ring 280 to 490 m from the center under a normal surface.

    Why each component is where it is, and dense or light:

    • The light torus (−425 kg/m³; ring radius 383 m, about the body's mean radius; tube radius 110 m) removes mass from the equatorial plane: it makes most of J2 (+478σ of C¯20) and nearly all of J4 (−92σ of C¯40). Its two radii are what make J2 and J4 in the measured proportion.
    • The dense core (+1200 kg/m³, 5 m from the center of figure) concentrates mass at the center: it adds to J2 (+133σ) and makes the small center-of-mass offset.
    • The dense layer (+780 kg/m³, 11 m) takes part of J2 back (−40σ): with the torus taking mass from the outer equator, the surface itself is denser than the background.

    All three take mass from the outer part of the equatorial plane, 270 m and more from the spin axis, and put it farther in: their excess and their deficit are both centered within 15 m of the center of figure, as for a structure symmetric about the spin axis. Where farther in differs. The model with components puts the mass in a dense core of 145 m radius at the center, under a dense surface layer; the search makes the whole interior inside its torus denser, under a light crust; the point masses put it toward the poles and, less, at the center. Cell by cell they correlate 0.37 to 0.57. The light equatorial ring is what the measurements fix (J2 and J4); how the mass inside it is arranged they leave open. The sector grids are far from consistent (section 3) and are not shown.
