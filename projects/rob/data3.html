    ROB-DATA3: Didymos#

    Last edited October 27, 2026

    Result. A dense core near the center of figure inside a thick, light equatorial torus explains every coefficient to degree 5 and the inertia: reduced χ2 1.22 (p=0.18). The core is 1200 ± 100 kg/m³ denser than the background and within 5 m of the center of figure; the torus, 383 m in radius and 110 m thick, is 425 ± 7 kg/m³ lighter. The same model, fitted to degrees 1–4, predicts degree 5, and it reproduces most of the anomaly of the exact values up to degree 7. Sector grids, which assume no structure, cannot fit this package at all. The package's mass is that of 2600 kg/m³.

best: core, placed; torus 1.05/0.30 r; layer

    What works and what does not:

    • A core alone cannot: at the center of figure it leaves a reduced χ2 of 3500, and placed freely it runs to 147 m above the center to reduce J4 instead of J2.
    • A core and a torus of the default size (radii 0.95 and 0.15 of the mean radius, 346 and 55 m) reach 13: the torus is needed, and its density, −660 kg/m³, is well determined, but its size is not right. Freeing its height and axis does not help (14): the torus is level and centered.
    • The torus size decides the fit. A search over the ring radius and the tube radius (0.80 to 1.10 and 0.10 to 0.30 of the mean radius) found the best at a ring radius of 1.05 and a tube radius of 0.30 of the mean radius: 383 m and 110 m, a torus that fills the equatorial region out to the surface. With it the reduced χ2 is 1.22.
    • The core's density depends on the torus size: +1450 kg/m³ with a 92 m tube, +1200 with 110 m, +840 with the 110 m tube at 365 m. The core and the torus share the central condensation; its total is fixed, the split is not.
    • The layer is not determined: −900 to +1400 kg/m³ across the models, with 1σ of about 250 kg/m³.
    • Sector grids of one or two shells, 4 latitude and 8 longitude bands, cannot fit (reduced χ2 860 and 1760): a dense core inside a light ring needs more radial resolution than two shells.
