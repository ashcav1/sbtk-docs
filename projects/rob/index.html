    Testing with ROB data#

    Last edited October 27, 2026

    The Royal Observatory of Belgium (ROB), one of the teams that make interiors for the HLP6 blind test, distributed six measurement packages of hidden interiors: three of Didymos (ROB-DATA1 to 3) and three of Dimorphos (ROB-DATA4 to 6). This section reads each of them with the procedure of Reading the gravity coefficients, fits four kinds of solution to the same measurements (models of components, a search of the geometry, sector grids and point masses), compares them on the same terms, and shows where they agree. The hidden interiors are not known to us; everything here comes from the measurements.

    The packages#

    Each package has

    • shgrav.txt: fully normalized gravity coefficients of degrees 0 to 10 about the origin of the DART shape-model file, in its axes, with ROB's reference radius and GM in the header;
    • moi.txt: the six inertia elements I/(MRref2) about the same origin;
    • for Dimorphos also the gravitational potential and acceleration at one point on the surface, as the GRASS gravimeter on a lander would measure them.

    The values are exact (ROB's 1σ columns are 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁶, far below what a mission measures). The headers give each package's mass and reference radius:

    Uncertainties#

    The packages are weighted with the precision of the Hera radio-science end-to-end simulation, as in the blind test (projects/hera_hlp6/rob_data.m, which calls hlp6_uncertainties.m; Didymos and Dimorphos values):

    • every coefficient's 1σ from the RSE gravity field, with its full covariance moved from the RSE center of mass to the origin of the shape-model file (Uncertainties and the expansion point) and scaled to the package's reference radius;
    • the coefficients truncated to the degrees the RSE field has: 5 for Didymos (35 coefficients) and 3 for Dimorphos (15, of which degree 3 has 1σ of about 10⁻², so that Dimorphos is measured to degree 2);
    • each inertia element's 1σ from the RSE inertia solution, with the variance the center of mass adds about the origin. For Didymos they are about 2.8 × 10⁻² of MR2 and carry no information; for Dimorphos about 5 × 10⁻⁴, and they do.

    How to read these pages#

    Every package page has the same sections, and every kind of solution is shown the same way.

    C − C_CD. Each measured coefficient minus the coefficient of the same shape with constant density (CD), both about the center of figure: what the interior adds to the gravity field. 0 is constant density. The bars are ±1σ of the Hera radio-science solution (Uncertainties). Zonal terms are shown as Jl=−C¯l0; degree 1 is the center-of-mass offset (C¯11,S¯11,C¯10=x,y,z over 3Rref). The figures show degrees 1 to 4 (Dimorphos: 1 to 3, of which degree 3 has a 1σ of about 10⁻², so that Dimorphos is measured to degree 2), each degree on its own scale, and the three moments of inertia.

    Reading the geometry. The sign of a coefficient of C − C_CD says where mass was added or taken away, as a mass excess at a point would make it: C¯10,C¯11,S¯11 the side the center of mass moved to; J2 higher, mass moved toward the equator; C¯22>0, toward the ends of the x-axis; C¯21>0, toward +x in the north and −x in the south; J3 higher, toward the south. sbtk.interpret.geometry combines the five coefficients of degree 2 into the axes mass moved toward and away from, and draws each degree as a map. Every component of a model is placed to make some of these; the contribution figure shows which.

    The kinds of solution. All are fitted to the same measurements (every coefficient the Hera uncertainties cover and Ixx,Iyy,Izz) and compared the same way:

    Kind What it assumes
    components a few shapes placed by reading the coefficients: a core, a torus, layers, spheres, polar caps, ellipsoids
    search a core, a torus and a layer with their sizes, axis and the core's position searched (Searching the geometry), and compact masses where they pass the likelihood-ratio test
    sectors 32 or 64 blocks of latitude, longitude and depth, each with its own density, smoothed (Tikhonov) as much as the measurements allow
    point masses a few thousand masses on a grid, smoothed in the same way

    χ², reduced χ², p. χ² is the sum over the measurements of ((solution − measured)/σ)², with the correlations of the coefficients taken into account. If the solution were the truth, χ² would be about the number of measurements minus the number of parameters fitted (ν, the degrees of freedom): the reduced χ² = χ²/ν is then about 1, and a value well above 1 means the solution misses something the measurements see. The p-value is the probability that the measurement noise alone gives a χ² this large; p ≥ 0.05 is called consistent: the misfit is no larger than the noise.

    Effective parameters. How many independent things the measurements determine in a solution (the trace of the data resolution matrix): 4 for a core with its position, 1 per density, fractions for smoothed sectors and point masses. A solution with more parameters fits more easily, so solutions are ranked by the Akaike information criterion, AIC = χ² + 2 × effective parameters: lower is better, and a parameter must lower χ² by more than 2 to pay for itself.

    Mean squared residual to degree 4. The average of ((solution − measured)/σ)² over the coefficients of degrees 1 to 4 alone, without the correlations: 1 is a match within 1σ on average. It is what the comparison figure shows coefficient by coefficient.

    The moments of inertia. Degree 2 holds only the differences of the moments of inertia (C¯20, C¯22, and the products of inertia in C¯21,S¯21,S¯22). The moments themselves add their sum, 2∫ρr2dV: how far the mass is from the center, which separates a dense core from a light surface (both change degree 2 alike). The Bennu analysis (Cavale et al.) fitted gravity alone, C¯10 to J4 with equal weights; here the full covariance of the Hera solution weights every coefficient to the degree it is measured, and the moments are added with their own 1σ. For Didymos that 1σ is 2.8 × 10⁻² of MRref2, larger than any change an interior makes, so the moments carry no information; for Dimorphos it is about 5 × 10⁻⁴, and they do.

    Exact values. ROB's packages give exact coefficients to degree 10. A solution fitted to the measured degrees is tested on them: the fraction of each degree it reproduces, 1 − |solution − exact|²/|exact|² (1: all of it; 0: no better than constant density; negative: worse).

    Method#

    For every package (projects/hera_hlp6/analyze_rob.m, then rob_figures.m for the figures and tables of the pages):

    1. Reading (sbtk.interpret.anomaly, sbtk.interpret.geometry): C − C_CD coefficient by coefficient, the center-of-mass offset, the axes of degree 2, each degree as a map.
    2. Models with components chosen from the reading (rob_candidates.m): a core (placed by its χ² profile along the center-of-mass offset, then by Gauss–Newton), layers, tori, ellipsoids, and for some packages polar caps and spheres where the reading put mass. The fits run on the 49,152-facet versions of the shape models with the measurements moved to them (sbtk.data.to_reduced_shape).
    3. The search (sbtk.search.run) of a core, a torus and a layer, and compact masses.
    4. Sector grids and point masses, smoothed as much as the measurements allow (the largest Tikhonov parameter with p ≥ 0.05).
    5. Comparison on the same terms (AIC, the coefficients one by one, ROB's exact values to degree 10), the contributions of each component of the best model, and the best consistent solution of each kind side by side.
    6. For Dimorphos, the GRASS surface point as an independent check.

    Across the six packages#

    • Reading the coefficients chose the components that win. In five of the six the best solution by AIC is a model of a few components placed where the reading of C − C_CD put mass: the two equatorial bodies of DATA1 at the ends of the axis degree 2 moves mass toward, the ring of DATA3 from its even degrees, the core of DATA4 and DATA5 on the side of the center-of-mass offset and the axis of degree 2, the light core and dense outside of DATA6 from the moments. Each page's contribution figure shows which coefficients each component makes.
    • Where no component of the candidate set has the right shape, the sector grids win. DATA2's anomaly is tilted and off center at once; cores, tori and layers stay far from it (reduced χ² 13 and more), the sector grids fit it, and the sector grids, the point masses and the search's core all point to the same side.
    • Point masses fit, but at a price, and never win. They are consistent in every package only with many effective parameters (24 to 32 of the 38 Didymos measurements), they leave the moments of DATA5 and DATA6 2–3σ off, and on interiors whose truth is known they explain none of the density at their own resolution (Point masses on the same interiors). Where the anomaly is large and to one side they show it (DATA1 to DATA3: cell by cell they correlate 0.46 to 0.79 with the best solutions of the other kinds); on Dimorphos they keep the mass nearly symmetric about the center (0.12 to 0.47).
    • The kinds agree on where the anomaly is, not on its form. Side by side, the best consistent solution of each kind puts the excess in the same place in DATA1 (the two equatorial ends, the −x end most), DATA2 (below the equator toward the south-west), DATA3 (inside a light equatorial ring) and DATA6 (light inside, dense outside). The sector grids and point masses spread it; the models with components make it compact. On DATA4 and DATA5 the smooth solutions keep the extra mass symmetric about the center where the models with components put one body to one side, and the coefficients cannot tell them apart: on DATA4 the GRASS point decides for the core, on DATA5 ROB's exact values to degree 10 favor the one sphere.
    • The moments matter for Dimorphos only. At the Hera precision the Didymos moments carry no information; for Dimorphos they are as decisive as degree 2 (DATA4, DATA6), and only the models with components reach them.
    • One surface point can decide, or not. On DATA4 the GRASS acceleration picks the model with the core 31 m toward −y (0.2%) over the smooth solutions (10%); on DATA5 the anomaly is too small for one point; on DATA6 it favors the smooth solutions in size and none of the solutions in direction. It tests the material under the lander, which the coefficients see only through degree 2.
