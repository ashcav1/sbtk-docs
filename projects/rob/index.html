    Testing with ROB data#

    Last edited October 27, 2026

    The Royal Observatory of Belgium (ROB), one of the teams that make interiors for the HLP6 blind test, distributed six measurement packages of hidden interiors: three of Didymos (ROB-DATA1 to 3) and three of Dimorphos (ROB-DATA4 to 6). This section reads each of them with the procedure of Reading the gravity coefficients, fits models of increasing detail, tests how many coefficients each model can fit, and shows where a model-free solution of thousands of point masses puts the density anomaly. The hidden interiors are not known to us; everything here comes from the measurements.

    The packages#

    Each package has

    • shgrav.txt: fully normalized gravity coefficients of degrees 0 to 10 about the origin of the DART shape-model file, in its axes, with ROB's reference radius and GM in the header;
    • moi.txt: the six inertia elements I/(MRref2) about the same origin;
    • for Dimorphos also the gravitational potential and acceleration at one point on the surface, as the GRASS gravimeter on a lander would measure them.

    The values are exact (ROB's 1σ columns are 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁶, far below what a mission measures). The headers give each package's mass and reference radius:

    Uncertainties#

    The packages are weighted with the precision of the Hera radio-science end-to-end simulation, as in the blind test (projects/hera_hlp6/rob_data.m, which calls hlp6_uncertainties.m; Didymos and Dimorphos values):

    • every coefficient's 1σ from the RSE gravity field, with its full covariance moved from the RSE center of mass to the origin of the shape-model file (Uncertainties and the expansion point) and scaled to the package's reference radius;
    • the coefficients truncated to the degrees the RSE field has: 5 for Didymos (35 coefficients) and 3 for Dimorphos (15, of which degree 3 has 1σ of about 10⁻², so that Dimorphos is measured to degree 2);
    • each inertia element's 1σ from the RSE inertia solution, with the variance the center of mass adds about the origin. For Didymos they are about 2.8 × 10⁻² of MR2 and carry no information; for Dimorphos about 5 × 10⁻⁴, and they do.

    Method#

    For every package:

    1. Reading (sbtk.interpret.anomaly, sbtk.plot.significance, sbtk.plot.degree_ratio): which measurements differ from constant density, the center-of-mass offset, each degree's scaling and the rest.
    2. Models of increasing detail, fitted to all the coefficients the uncertainties cover: a core at the center of figure; a core placed by the χ2 profile along the center-of-mass offset and then by Gauss–Newton; with a surface layer; with a torus about the z-axis, its height and axis free; an ellipsoidal core with free position and axes. The fits run on the 49,152-facet versions of the shape models with the measurements moved to them (sbtk.data.to_reduced_shape).
    3. Truncation: the best model fitted to degrees 1–L for each L, predicting the rest (sbtk.invert.predict), and the fraction of each degree's anomaly to degree 10 it reproduces.
    4. Point masses (sbtk.interior.sphere_grid, sbtk.interpret.point_masses): a few thousand point masses with the smallest weighted norm that fits the measurements, and where their positive and negative parts concentrate.
    5. For Dimorphos, the surface point: the potential and acceleration of the homogeneous body and of the best model against the given values (sbtk.gravity.field_at).

    projects/hera_hlp6/analyze_rob.m runs every step for one package, with the candidate models of rob_candidates.m; rob_surface_point.m adds a buried sphere under the Dimorphos surface point.

    Results#

    Package What the reading says Best model (consistent with the measurements) Reduced χ2 How far it reaches
    ROB-DATA1 degree 2 scaled up 5.7%, C¯22 +33σ: mass toward the equator, elongated light polar caps, two dense bodies at the equator along −24°/156°, a light region at depth; or 32 sectors 0.83 degrees 1–4; sectors fail beyond 4
    ROB-DATA2 center of mass 2.3 m off, degree 3 +3.7%: lateral structure 32 sectors: a denser south toward −x, a lighter north and +y 1.65 degree 5; 27% of degree 6
    ROB-DATA3 degree 2 −12%, degree 4 −35%, even degrees down: a core and a ring dense core (+1200 kg/m³) at the center, light thick torus (−425 kg/m³, 383 m) 1.22 degree 7 of the exact values
    ROB-DATA4 C¯22 changes sign: mass along ±y dense core 31–36 m toward −y, torus, dense crust 0.3–0.65 degree 2
    ROB-DATA5 small: C¯22 +27σ, inertia 3–5σ lower one sphere +293 kg/m³, 27 m toward +x 0.03 every degree to 10 (38–99%)
    ROB-DATA6 degree 2 +11%, inertia +30 to +60σ: mass outward light core 54 m toward −x, dense torus, dense crust 0.39 degree 2

    What worked, across the six:

    • Reading first chose the right family every time: even degrees reduced pointed to the torus of DATA3, a large degree 3 with a large center-of-mass offset to lateral structure in DATA2, a scaled-up degree 2 and inertia to mass moved outward in DATA6.
    • Placing the core by its χ2 profile along the center-of-mass offset chose the side in every package: a dense core on the side of the offset (DATA2, DATA4, DATA5) or a light one opposite it (DATA1, DATA6), where the other side fits much worse. Gauss–Newton from the center of figure alone can end on the wrong side (Reading the gravity coefficients).
    • The size of a component matters as much as its density: DATA3 went from reduced χ2 13 to 1.2 only by searching the torus radii, which SBTK does not estimate.
    • Sector grids without assumed structure fit the packages whose structure is lateral (DATA1, DATA2, DATA4) and fail where it is radial (DATA3; DATA6 with one shell), which is itself a reading. Their straight edges add structure at high degree that the data do not have.
    • Point masses placed the anomalies in the right regions in every package (the dense lower −y region of DATA2, the dense center of DATA3, the light centers of DATA1 and DATA6) but cannot form shapes such as a ring.

    What did not:

    • At the radio-science precision, Dimorphos is measured to degree 2 and the inertia: 8 numbers that several different interiors fit equally well. The surface point decides between them: for DATA4 a dense core toward −y with a dense crust matches the lander's acceleration to 0.2%, the smoothest sector model misses it by 10%.
    • Free geometry runs away when the model lacks structure the data contain (a core alone in DATA3 went to 147 m above the center), as Estimated geometry describes.
