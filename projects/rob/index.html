    Testing with ROB data#

    Last edited October 29, 2026

    The Royal Observatory of Belgium (ROB), one of the teams that make interiors for the HLP6 blind test, distributed six measurement packages of hidden interiors: three of Didymos (ROB-DATA1 to 3) and three of Dimorphos (ROB-DATA4 to 6). This section reads each package with the procedure of Reading the gravity coefficients and fits three kinds of solution to the same measurements: a model with components chosen from that reading, a sector grid and point masses. Each is made by its own script (Code of the ROB solutions). It then tests every solution on what it was not fitted to: ROB's exact coefficients to degree 10 and, for Dimorphos, the gravity at one point on the surface. The hidden interiors are not known to us.

    The packages#

    Each package has

    • shgrav.txt: fully normalized gravity coefficients of degrees 0 to 10 about the origin of the DART shape-model file, in its axes, with ROB's reference radius and GM in the header;
    • moi.txt: the six inertia elements I/(MRref2) about the same origin;
    • for Dimorphos also the gravitational potential and acceleration at one point on the surface, as the GRASS gravimeter on a lander would measure them.

    The values are exact (ROB's 1σ columns are 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻⁶, far below what a mission measures). The headers give each package's mass and reference radius:

    Uncertainties#

    The packages are weighted with the precision of the Hera radio-science end-to-end simulation, as in the blind test (projects/hera_hlp6/rob_data.m, which calls hlp6_uncertainties.m; Didymos and Dimorphos values):

    • every coefficient's 1σ from the RSE gravity field, with its full covariance moved from the RSE center of mass to the origin of the shape-model file (Uncertainties and the expansion point) and scaled to the package's reference radius;
    • the coefficients truncated to the degrees the RSE field has, and every one of them fitted: degrees 1 to 5 for Didymos (35 coefficients) and 1 to 3 for Dimorphos (15). The fits do not use C¯00: the mass is the package's. For Dimorphos the 1σ of degree 3, about 3 × 10⁻², is 20 to 600 times its anomaly, so degree 3 is fitted but constrains nothing. For Didymos degree 5 is resolved on ROB-DATA2 and 3 (up to 6σ) and not on ROB-DATA1;
    • each inertia element's 1σ from the RSE inertia solution, with the variance the center of mass adds about the origin. For Didymos they are about 2.8 × 10⁻² of MR2 and carry no information; for Dimorphos about 5 × 10⁻⁴, and they do.

    What the six packages show#

    • A model with a few components, chosen by reading the coefficients, fits with the fewest parameters. It ranks first by AIC on ROB-DATA1, 3, 4 and 5: by 37 and 18 on Didymos, by 4.6 on ROB-DATA4, and by 1.9 on ROB-DATA5, a tie with the point masses. On ROB-DATA6 it ties with the point masses and the sector grid (all within 3). On ROB-DATA2 no model of a core, a torus and a layer fits; a dense and a light sphere with four compact masses does, and ties with the sector grid and the point masses.
    • Fitting best is not the same as being right above the fitted degrees. There ROB's exact values side with the point masses on ROB-DATA2, 3 and 6, and with the model with components on ROB-DATA1, 4 and 5. The degrees the measurements resolve, to 4 or 5 for Didymos and to 2 for Dimorphos, fix where the anomaly is; they do not fix how compact it is.
    • Given no shapes, the point masses often find the geometry of the model with components. On ROB-DATA1 they find its light column along the spin axis and put the mass at the two ends of one equatorial axis. On ROB-DATA3 they put a dense center inside a light equatorial ring. On ROB-DATA4 they put the excess toward −y, where the core is. On ROB-DATA5 they find the core's size and elongation but not its side. Where they agree with the model with components, the measurements, not the choice of shapes, placed the anomaly.
    • Where the two differ, the measurements allow both, and the truth goes either way. On ROB-DATA5 the core's offset to +x, which the point masses leave out because degree 1 is measured at only 1σ, is right. On ROB-DATA6 the point masses' light region at the center is closer to the truth than the light core to one side.
    • Sector grids fit an anomaly spread over a direction and depth (ROB-DATA2), but cannot make a ring (ROB-DATA3). Above the fitted degrees they do worse than constant density on ROB-DATA1, 3 and 5.
    • For Dimorphos the moments of inertia and one surface point matter. At the Hera precision the moments carry no information for Didymos. For Dimorphos they are as strong as degree 2 (ROB-DATA4 and 6). The GRASS point decides where it lies over the anomaly: on ROB-DATA4 the compact core predicts it 3.7% too high and the point masses 9.3% too low, where constant density is 13.3% low.

    Neither weighting is better everywhere. The AIC of the two differs by less than 4 on every package. Above the fitted degrees sensitivity weighting is far better on ROB-DATA1 and better on ROB-DATA5, equal weighting far better on ROB-DATA4 and better on ROB-DATA6; on ROB-DATA2 and 3 they are close. The pages show sensitivity weighting, which spreads the anomaly over depth instead of putting it under the surface; each page's weighting.md table in the results folder has both.

    How to read these pages#

    Every package page has the same six sections: what the measurements show, where the mass is, how well each solution fits, the model with components, the solutions side by side, and the test against ROB's exact values (for Dimorphos also against the GRASS point).

    C − C_CD. Each measured coefficient minus the coefficient of the same shape at constant density (CD), both about the center of figure: what the interior adds to the gravity field. 0 is constant density. The bars are ±1σ of the Hera radio-science solution (Uncertainties). Zonal terms are shown as Jl=−C¯l0; degree 1 is the center-of-mass offset (C¯11,S¯11,C¯10=x,y,z over 3Rref). The sign of a coefficient says where mass was added or taken away: J2 higher, toward the equator; C¯22>0, toward the ends of the x-axis; C¯21>0, toward +x in the north and −x in the south (Reading the gravity coefficients).

    The maps. Each resolved degree of C − C_CD, up to the highest with a measurement more than 3σ from constant density, drawn as a pattern on the sphere: red where a mass added near the surface in that direction would produce it, blue where a mass taken away would (sbtk.plot.anomaly_map).

    The kinds of solution. All are fitted to the same measurements, every coefficient the Hera uncertainties cover and Ixx,Iyy,Izz:

    Kind What it assumes Page
    Components a few shapes chosen by reading the coefficients: a core, a torus, layers, spheres, ellipsoids, polar caps; their densities and some positions fitted, sizes fixed or searched on a grid, compact masses added where they pass the likelihood-ratio test Models with components, Searching the geometry
    Sector grid 32 or 64 blocks of latitude, longitude and depth, each with its own density Sector grids
    Point masses about 2,700 masses on a grid Point masses

    Relative density. In the side-by-side figures, the density minus the bulk density, so that every kind is drawn on the same scale. In the component tables and the three-dimensional views, a component's density minus the background density, the quantity the fit estimates.

    Reduced χ² and p. χ² is the sum over the measurements of ((solution − measured)/σ)², with the correlations of the coefficients. The reduced χ² divides it by the degrees of freedom; about 1 means the solution fits to the noise. p is the probability that noise alone gives a χ² this large; p ≥ 0.05 is consistent with the measurements. The measurements are ROB's exact values weighted with the Hera uncertainties, without noise added, so a reduced χ² well below 1, as for every solution on Dimorphos, means a solution matches them more closely than the uncertainties require.

    Parameters and AIC. The number of parameters is how many independent quantities a solution takes from the measurements: one per density or coordinate of a model with components, and one per size chosen on a grid; for the regularized sector grids and point masses, the effective number, the trace of the data resolution matrix. The Akaike information criterion, AIC = χ² + 2 × effective parameters, ranks solutions of different kinds on the same terms: lower is better, a difference below 2 is a tie, above 10 a strong preference (Goodness of fit). Sector grids and point masses are regularized with the parameter of lowest AIC.

    Exact values. ROB's packages give exact coefficients to degree 10; the solutions are fitted to degrees 1 to 5 for Didymos and 1 to 3 for Dimorphos. Each solution is tested on all ten: the fraction of each degree it reproduces, 1 − |solution − exact|²/|exact|². 1 is all of it, 0 no better than constant density, negative worse. The degrees above the fitted ones, 6 to 10 for Didymos and 4 to 10 for Dimorphos, are a test on coefficients no solution was fitted to.

    GRASS. For Dimorphos, ROB gives the acceleration at one point on the surface, as the GRASS gravimeter on a lander would measure it. Each solution's prediction is compared in magnitude and direction.

    Method#

    For every package the runners in projects/hera_hlp6/rob/ fit the three solutions and make the figures and tables of its page (Code of the ROB solutions); projects/hera_hlp6/analyze_rob.m chose their models:

    1. Reading (sbtk.interpret.anomaly, sbtk.interpret.geometry): C − C_CD coefficient by coefficient, the center-of-mass offset, the axes of degree 2, each degree as a map.
    2. Models with components chosen from the reading (rob_candidates.m): a core placed by its χ² profile along the center-of-mass offset and then by Gauss–Newton, layers, tori, ellipsoids, a light column along the spin axis, and polar caps and spheres where the reading put mass; and two searches (sbtk.search.run): a core, a torus and a layer with their sizes on a grid, and a dense and a light sphere started where the point masses put the mass; both with compact masses added while they pass the likelihood-ratio test. The model of lowest AIC is the package's model with components. The fits run on the 49,152-facet versions of the shape models with the measurements moved to them (sbtk.data.to_reduced_shape).
    3. Sector grids (1 × 4 × 8 and 2 × 4 × 8; the one of lower AIC) and point masses (both weightings), each with the regularization parameter of lowest AIC.
    4. Comparison of the three solutions, coefficient by coefficient, on two planes through the body, against ROB's exact values to degree 10, and for Dimorphos at the GRASS surface point.
