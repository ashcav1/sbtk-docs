    ROB-DATA1: Didymos#

    Last edited October 28, 2026

    Degree 2 of ROB-DATA1 moves mass from the spin axis toward the two ends of one equatorial axis, and degree 1 says that one end carries more than the other. A model of five components built on that reading (light polar caps, a dense sphere at each end, a light core below the center) fits every measured coefficient with 14 parameters. The sector grid and the point masses fit as well but need twice as many, and rank 14 behind in AIC. The point masses arrive at the same picture without being told any shapes: mass at the two equatorial ends, more at the −x end, and less along the spin axis. Beyond the measured degrees ROB's exact values side with the point masses, which spread the dense material over a larger volume than two spheres.

    The measurements more than 3σ from constant density.

    Three things stand out. J2 is 246σ higher than at constant density: mass has moved from the spin axis toward the equator. C¯22 and S¯22 say the move is not even around the equator but toward one axis. And the center of mass is 0.59 m from the center of figure toward −x: one end of that axis is heavier. Degrees 3 and 4 are small but measured. The moments of inertia are not: for Didymos their 1σ is larger than any interior changes them.

    2. Where the mass is#

    Degree 2 puts the excess on the equator at longitudes −19° and 161° (±2°) and the deficit at both poles. The center of mass points to longitude −167°, 12° from the 161° end. Degree 3 has its strongest excess at −172° and degree 4 at −18° and 162°: the same axis, the same heavier end.

    So the model built for this package has a dense sphere at each end of that axis, started 250 m out along −20° and 160°, a light cap at each pole (the parts of the body above 45° and below −45° latitude), and a core of free position and density for any remaining deficit near the center.

    3. How well each solution fits#

    The model with components, the sector grid and the point masses all match every measured coefficient within 2σ. They differ in what it costs: 14 effective parameters for the components, about 28 for the others, which is why the components rank first by 14 in AIC, a strong preference. The search of a core, a torus and a layer cannot fit (reduced χ² 4.3): a torus about the spin axis cannot put mass at two ends of one equatorial axis.

    Background density 2622 kg/m³ (bulk density 2603 kg/m³). Makes most of: the component's part of each coefficient in units of its 1σ.

    Each component does what the reading asked of it:

    • The west and east spheres (+1880 and +1130 kg/m³, 235 and 251 m from the center at longitudes 167° and −24°) make C¯22 and S¯22. Being unequal, they also move the center of mass toward −x. The west sphere is at the density bound of 4500 kg/m³: the excess at the 161° end is more than a sphere of 73 m radius can hold.
    • The light north cap (−179 kg/m³) and the light core (−1330 kg/m³, 155 m below the center) take mass from near the spin axis and make most of J2. In C¯10 they balance: the cap alone would move the center of mass south, the core alone north.
    • The south cap (+112 ± 65 kg/m³) is barely resolved.

    The point masses were given no shapes, yet they find the same structure as the model with components. Both take mass from along the spin axis: the point masses as a light column through the center, the components as a light north cap and a light core below the center. Both add it at the equator, more on the −x side: the excess of each is centered 34 to 35 m toward −x. The difference is how compact the dense material is. The components gather it into two spheres; the point masses spread it over the outer equator. Their relative densities correlate 0.51 cell by cell.

    The sector grid is the weakest of the three here. Its outer shell has light polar caps and slightly denser equatorial sectors at the longitudes of the axis, but its inner shell is not resolved (1σ of about 850 kg/m³). It correlates about 0.27 with each of the others.

    6. Against ROB's exact values#

    All three reproduce 84 to 100% of each of degrees 1 to 4. Beyond them they part. The point masses reproduce 87% of degree 5, and over degrees 5 to 10 they do as well as constant density (−0.03). The two compact spheres overshoot degrees 5, 7 and 9 (−1.49 over degrees 5 to 10), and the sector grid more so (−2.89). The dense material at the two ends of the axis is real, but it is spread over a larger volume than two spheres of 73 m radius. To degree 4 the spheres describe it; beyond degree 4, the point masses describe it better.
