    SBTK: Small Body Toolkit#

    Last edited October 30, 2026

    SBTK is a MATLAB toolkit for the interior density distribution of asteroids and other small bodies. It computes the gravity coefficients, center of mass and moments of inertia of a density model inside a polyhedral shape model, and it estimates the densities of such a model from measured gravity coefficients and moments of inertia. This site documents version 1.0.0.

    Capabilities#

    The following are implemented and covered by the test suite (Validation).

    Density models
    A background density, uniform components (spheres, triaxial ellipsoids, tori with any axis, layers below the surface, latitude–longitude–radius sectors) and an optional Gaussian random density field, clipped to a closed polyhedral shape model that is star-shaped about the expansion origin (Density distributions).
    Gravity and moment calculations
    Fully normalized spherical-harmonic coefficients to any degree, the center of mass and the inertia tensor of such a model, from exact polyhedral integrals over the cones between the expansion origin and each surface facet (Gravity and moment calculations).
    Potential and acceleration at points
    The gravitational potential and acceleration outside, on or inside the body: exact for a homogeneous polyhedron and for spheres inside the body, and for the other departures from the background density on a grid of uniform cubes (Potential and acceleration at points).
    Frames
    Coefficients about the center of mass in the shape-model axes, the spin axes or the principal axes of inertia, and bodies, measurements and models carried into another frame (Frames of a gravity field).
    Measurement sets
    Gravity coefficients with a full covariance, moment-of-inertia elements, linear combinations of them, the libration ratio (B−A)/C and the center of mass, simulated (exact values, or with uncertainties and Gaussian noise) or read from files (Measurement sets).
    Reading the coefficients
    The measured coefficients minus those of the homogeneous body, about the center of figure, read as the center-of-mass offset, the principal axes of degree 2 and each degree's directions of excess and deficit; the anomaly each candidate component makes, and a forward stepwise selection of candidates (Reading the gravity coefficients).
    Density estimation
    Weighted least squares for the background density and the relative density of each component, with the total mass as an equality constraint, bounds on the densities, optional Tikhonov regularization (its parameter by the discrepancy principle or the lowest Akaike information criterion), optional estimation of the position of spheres, ellipsoids and tori and of the axis of ellipsoids and tori, linearized 1σ uncertainties, and a χ2 test of the fit (Density estimation).
    Kinds of solution
    Models with components; a search of their geometry (every combination of given sizes, positions and axes, then Gauss–Newton, then compact masses kept by the likelihood-ratio test); sector grids that fill the body; and regularized point masses (Models with components, Searching the geometry, Sector grids, Point masses).
    Comparison with a true model
    For synthetic tests: the fraction of the variance of the true density explained by the estimate (R2), the largest value the chosen components allow, and a cell-by-cell comparison of density grids (Goodness of fit and model comparison).
    Exchange packages
    Plain-text packages of measurements, interior models and solutions with their units, frame and normalization, for comparisons between teams (Exchange packages).

    The assumptions behind these calculations and what SBTK does not do are listed in Assumptions and limitations.

    Status and requirements#

    SBTK 1.0.0 has not been publicly released; its source repository is private. It requires MATLAB R2024b or newer with the Optimization Toolbox and no other. The polyhedral gravity kernel is a compiled C function, provided for macOS and buildable on other platforms (Installation).

    Credits#

    SBTK was written by A. Cavale (University of Colorado Boulder). The polyhedral gravity kernel ComputePolyhedralCSBar_mex was written by Y. Takahashi (University of Colorado Boulder). The Bennu project reproduces the inversion of Cavale et al., "Constraints on the Internal Density Distribution of Bennu Using OSIRIS-REx Mission Data" (Icarus). Shape models: DART stereophotoclinometry models of Didymos (v003) and Dimorphos (v004); OSIRIS-REx shape model of Bennu.
