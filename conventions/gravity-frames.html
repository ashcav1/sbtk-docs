    Frames of a gravity field#

    A set of gravity coefficients is defined only together with the point it is expanded about and the axes its latitude and longitude are measured in. Changing the point or the axes changes every coefficient but not the potential outside the body. This page gives the two changes used for small bodies, moving the expansion point to the center of mass and rotating the axes into the principal axes of inertia, what each makes zero, what the coefficients in each frame mean, and how the frame affects a comparison with a homogeneous body.

    Frame Expansion point Axes Coefficients that vanish
    Shape-model origin origin of the shape-model file shape-model axes none
    Center of mass, shape-model axes center of mass shape-model axes C¯10, C¯11, S¯11
    Center of mass, principal axes center of mass principal axes of inertia degree 1, and C¯21, S¯21, S¯22

    SBTK computes, measures and estimates in the shape-model axes about the center of mass (the second row, Coordinate systems). sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame gives any field in the second or third frame, and sbtk.plot.difference_from_homogeneous compares fields with the homogeneous body in either.

    Moving the expansion point to the center of mass#

    Degree 1#

    About an expansion point O, with d=(xc,yc,zc) the center of mass minus O, the degree-1 coefficients are (Gravity coefficients)

    C¯10=zc3Rref,C¯11=xc3Rref,S¯11=yc3Rref,

    so they vanish about the center of mass and only there. A radio-science field is expanded about the center of mass because the spacecraft orbit is integrated about it; the position of the center of mass relative to the shape model is then a separate result, not part of the coefficients.

    Higher degrees#

    Moving the expansion point changes the coefficients of every degree. The coefficients of degree l about the new point are a linear combination of those of degree ≤l about the old point, with powers of the displacement as weights: lower degrees enter higher ones, never the reverse. For degree 2 this is the parallel-axis theorem. With IO=Icm+M(|d|21−dd𝖳) and the relations between the inertia tensor and the degree-2 coefficients (Moments of inertia), the unnormalized coefficients about O are

    C20O=C20+2zc2−xc2−yc22R2,C21O=C21+xczcR2,S21O=S21+yczcR2, C22O=C22+xc2−yc24R2,S22O=S22+xcyc2R2,

    where C2m, S2m are about the center of mass. The change is of second order in |d|/R. For the Didymos model below, the shape-model origin is 55 m from the center of mass (|d|/Rref = 0.13) and C¯21 changes from 3.53×10−3 about the center of mass to 1.46×10−3 about the origin.

    SBTK applies the translation to all degrees at once with a matrix built from the solid harmonics of point masses, which is exact to rounding because the map is linear and finite (sbtk.internal.sh_translation). The covariance of a measurement set is propagated through the same matrix.

    Rotating into the principal axes#

    Principal axes from the degree-2 coefficients#

    About the center of mass, the degree-2 coefficients fix the inertia tensor up to a multiple of the identity (MacCullagh's formula). With unnormalized coefficients,

    I−Izz1MRref2=(C20−2C22−2S22−C21−2S22C20+2C22−S21−C21−S210).

    Its eigenvectors are the principal axes of inertia and its eigenvalues the principal moments A≤B≤C minus Izz, divided by MRref2. SBTK orders the principal axes as x′ along the axis of the smallest moment, y′ of the middle and z′ of the largest, points each to the positive side of the shape-model axis nearest to it, and makes the frame right-handed (sbtk.internal.principal_axes). An eigenvector has no sign, so a field in principal axes must state these choices: reversing x′ changes the sign of every coefficient whose value is odd in x, such as C¯31. When two moments are equal (a body symmetric about an axis) the axes in their plane are not defined.

    What the rotation changes#

    A rotation mixes the 2l+1 coefficients of each degree among themselves and no others; for fully normalized coefficients the mixing matrix of each degree is orthogonal, so the power of each degree,

    σl2=∑m=0l(C¯lm2+S¯lm2),

    is unchanged. In the principal axes the products of inertia vanish, so

    C¯21=S¯21=S¯22=0,C20=A+B−2C2MR2,C22=B−A4MR2  (unnormalized),

    and the whole degree-2 power is in C¯20 and C¯22. Degree 1 stays zero. The coefficients of degree 3 and higher are not made zero by any rotation; they change values but keep their power. SBTK builds the rotation matrix of each degree from the solid harmonics of points and their rotated images (sbtk.internal.sh_rotation), exact to rounding, and propagates a covariance through it.

    Rotating the coefficients is the same as computing the field of the rotated body: the homogeneous field of the shape model with its vertices rotated into the principal axes equals the rotated homogeneous coefficients to 10−12 (testDifferenceInPrincipalAxes, Validation).

    MATLAB script: A field in the three frames
    run("/path/to/SBTK/sbtk_setup.m")
body = sbtk.body.from_mass("Didymos", sbtk.shape.bundled("didymos"), 5.273e11, 0.43);
interior = sbtk.interior.create([sbtk.interior.sphere("core", [0 0 0], 0.170, +450), ...
                                 sbtk.interior.sphere("boulder", sbtk.interior.latlon(25, 60, 0.250), 0.040, +900)]);
[exact, truth] = sbtk.data.simulate(body, interior, 4, "frames");

pk = sbtk.internal.sh_packing(4);
about_origin = truth.coef*sbtk.internal.sh_translation(-truth.origin_km, pk, body.R_ref_km)';
cs = sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame(truth, body, "shape");        % degree 1 = 0
cp = sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame(truth, body, "principal");    % and C21 = S21 = S22 = 0
disp(table(cs.labels(1:8)', about_origin(2:9)', cs.coef(1:8)', cp.coef(1:8)', 'VariableNames', ...
           ["coefficient" "about the origin" "center of mass" "principal axes"]))
power = @(c, l) sum(c(pk.deg(2:end) == l).^2);
fprintf("degree-2 power: %.6e (shape axes), %.6e (principal axes)\n", power(cs.coef, 2), power(cp.coef, 2))
fprintf("principal axes in the shape-model axes (columns x', y', z'):\n");  disp(cp.principal)
fprintf("principal moments minus their mean, I/(M R^2): %.5f %.5f %.5f\n", cp.moments)

    Comparison with the homogeneous body#

    sbtk.plot.difference_from_homogeneous subtracts the field of the homogeneous body (density M/V) from a field, both about their own center of mass. With frame = "shape" both are in the shape-model axes. With "principal" both are rotated into the principal axes of the field, so the field's own C¯21, S¯21, S¯22 are zero and the differences in these three terms are minus the homogeneous body's terms in those axes. These terms measure the angles between the principal axes of the interior and those of the homogeneous body: for small angles, a rotation εy about y′ gives the homogeneous body a product of inertia |Ixz|≈(C−A)|εy|, so

    |C¯21|≈C−A5/3MR2|εy|,|S¯21|≈C−B5/3MR2|εx|,|S¯22|≈B−A25/12MR2|εz|.

    A mass excess m at (x,y,z) adds the products of inertia −mxz, −myz and −mxy: one at mid-latitude away from the z-axis tilts the axis of the largest moment, and one in a direction between the x′- and y′-axes turns the axes in the equatorial plane. The second output of the plot gives the tilt and the turn (axes_deg). In the example the true model's axis of the largest moment is 0.18° from the homogeneous body's; with (C−A)/(MR2)=0.083 this gives |C¯21|≈2×10−4, the value in the figure below, twice the 1σ of 10−4 assumed for degree 2 in the example.

    The plot carries the same information in either frame. In the shape-model axes the misalignment of the principal axes appears as differences in C¯21, S¯21, S¯22 that contain both fields' terms; in the field's principal axes it is moved entirely into the homogeneous body's terms, which is how a field given in its principal axes compares with a homogeneous model. The degree-1 points are in both cases the offset of the center of mass from the center of figure, in the axes of the plot.

    Frames of published fields#

    Two conventions are in use for measured and modeled fields of small bodies. Neither depends on whether the terms can be measured; they differ in how the x-axis is defined.

    Frame z-axis x-axis C¯21, S¯21 S¯22
    Body-fixed (cartographic) frame spin pole prime meridian, fixed by a surface feature 0 when the spin axis is the axis of the largest moment not 0 in general
    Principal axes axis of the largest moment axis of the smallest moment 0 0
    Shape-model axes (SBTK) z of the shape model, taken as the spin axis x of the shape model not 0 in general not 0 in general

    A body in principal-axis rotation, the relaxed state that energy dissipation leads to, spins about its axis of the largest moment; spin about any other axis would make the body wobble. In a body-fixed frame of such a body, z is a principal axis and C¯21=S¯21=0. The x-axis, however, is set by a surface feature through the prime meridian, not by the mass distribution, so S¯22 is not zero and the axes in the equatorial plane follow from the ratio of S¯22 to C¯22: the axis of the smallest moment lies at longitude

    λ22=12atan2(S¯22,C¯22).

    The OSIRIS-REx gravity field of Bennu (JPL, 2019) is of this kind: it is given in Bennu's body-fixed frame, defined by the spin pole and the prime meridian; C¯21 and S¯21 are ~10−14, zero to rounding, so they were set to zero rather than estimated, while C¯22=3.06×10−3 and S¯22=−1.09×10−3 put the axis of the smallest moment at λ22=−9.8°. Rotating by this angle about z would make S¯22 zero but would separate the field from the frame of the shape model, the maps and the navigation, without adding information, since the angle is already given by S¯22/C¯22.

    A field in principal axes has all three terms zero; this frame is natural for rotational dynamics and is common for model fields. Its relation to the shape model is the rotation between the two frames, which must be given with the field.

    All three frames carry the same information when the rotation and the expansion point relative to the shape model are known. The terms that are made zero are not lost: they become the rotation (three angles) and the center of mass (three coordinates).

    True models and the spin axis#

    The shape-model z-axis is taken as the spin axis. If a true model is meant to spin about it in principal-axis rotation, as observed bodies do, its axis of the largest moment should coincide with z, that is, C¯21=S¯21=0 about the center of mass in the shape-model axes. A density model does not satisfy this by itself: the homogeneous Didymos (DART v003) has its axis of the largest moment 3.6° from the shape-model z-axis and the example true model 3.7°. The value for a homogeneous body depends on the shape model and on the accuracy of its pole; a non-uniform interior moves the axis further or brings it closer to z. The interior-model package (sbtk.exchange.write_model) gives the principal axes of the true model as unit vectors in the shape-model axes and its principal moments, so that the true field can be stated in either frame and its tilt from the spin axis is on record.

    Functions#

    Function Frame
    sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame Any measurement set, true model or inversion result about its center of mass, in the shape-model axes or its principal axes, with the principal axes, the principal moments and the propagated covariance.
