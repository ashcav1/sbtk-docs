    Frames of a gravity field#

    Last edited October 20, 2026

    A set of gravity coefficients means nothing without the point it is expanded about and the axes its latitude and longitude are measured in. Changing the point or the axes changes every coefficient but not the potential outside the body. This page covers:

    • the two changes used for small bodies: moving the expansion point to the center of mass, and turning the axes into the spin axes or the principal axes of inertia;
    • what each change makes zero;
    • the frames used for past measured and modeled fields;
    • how the frame affects a comparison with a homogeneous body;
    • which frame a team that makes data sets (maker) or estimates interiors from them (finder) should use, including for a body that may be tumbling, such as Dimorphos.
    Frame Expansion point Axes Coefficients that vanish
    Shape-model origin origin of the shape-model file shape-model axes none
    Center of mass, shape-model axes center of mass shape-model axes C¯10, C¯11, S¯11
    Center of mass, spin axes center of mass z along the axis of the largest moment, x toward the shape-model x-axis degree 1, and C¯21, S¯21
    Center of mass, principal axes center of mass principal axes of inertia degree 1, and C¯21, S¯21, S¯22

    In SBTK, coefficients are always expanded about the center of mass, in the axes of the shape model in use. The functions that work with frames are:

    • sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame gives any field in the shape-model, spin or principal axes, with its principal axes and moments.
    • sbtk.plot.difference_from_homogeneous compares fields with the homogeneous body in any of the three.
    • sbtk.body.to_frame, sbtk.data.to_frame and sbtk.interior.to_frame carry a body, its measurements and its interior or true model into another frame. Every later step (simulation, packages, density grids, plots and comparisons) then works in that frame.

    Summary#

    • The information is the same in every frame. The terms a frame makes zero become its definition: three coordinates of the center of mass and up to three angles. Both must be known relative to the shape model.
    • Spin axes reproduce fields measured by radio science from a body that rotates about its axis of the largest moment (OSIRIS-REx at Bennu): C¯21=S¯21=0, but S¯22≠0 because the x-axis is set by a surface feature, not by the mass distribution.
    • Principal axes suit rotational dynamics and bodies without a fixed spin axis (tumbling), and also a synchronous satellite.
    • Makers should send the measurements, the shape-model file, the true model and the density grid in one frame: spin axes for a body like Didymos, principal axes for Dimorphos (Which frame to use).
    • Finders should work in the frame of the package they receive and report in it.
    • Against a homogeneous body, the frame-independent results are the angles between the principal axes of the field and of the homogeneous body (axes_deg) and the power of each degree.

    Moving the expansion point to the center of mass#

    Degree 1#

    About an expansion point O, with d=(xc,yc,zc) the center of mass minus O, the degree-1 coefficients are (Gravity coefficients)

    C¯10=zc3Rref,C¯11=xc3Rref,S¯11=yc3Rref,

    so they vanish about the center of mass and only there. A radio-science field is expanded about the center of mass because the spacecraft orbit is integrated about it. The position of the center of mass relative to the shape model is then a separate result, not part of the coefficients.

    Higher degrees#

    Moving the expansion point changes the coefficients of every degree. The coefficients of degree l about the new point are a linear combination of those of degree ≤l about the old point, weighted by powers of the displacement: lower degrees enter higher ones, never the reverse. For degree 2 this is the parallel-axis theorem. With IO=Icm+M(|d|21−dd𝖳) and the relations between the inertia tensor and the degree-2 coefficients (Moments of inertia), the unnormalized coefficients about O are

    C20O=C20+2zc2−xc2−yc22R2,C21O=C21+xczcR2,S21O=S21+yczcR2, C22O=C22+xc2−yc24R2,S22O=S22+xcyc2R2,

    where C2m, S2m are about the center of mass. The change is of second order in |d|/R. For the Didymos model below, the shape-model origin is 57 m from the center of mass (|d|/Rref = 0.13). As a result, C¯21 is 3.67×10−3 about the center of mass but 1.49×10−3 about the origin.

    SBTK applies the translation to all degrees at once with a matrix built from the solid harmonics of point masses (sbtk.internal.sh_translation). The map is linear and finite, so the result is exact to rounding. The covariance of a measurement set is propagated through the same matrix.

    Rotating the axes#

    Principal axes from the degree-2 coefficients#

    About the center of mass, the degree-2 coefficients fix the inertia tensor up to a multiple of the identity (MacCullagh's formula). With unnormalized coefficients,

    I−Izz1MRref2=(C20−2C22−2S22−C21−2S22C20+2C22−S21−C21−S210).

    Its eigenvectors are the principal axes of inertia. Its eigenvalues are the principal moments A≤B≤C minus Izz, divided by MRref2. SBTK orders the principal axes so that:

    • x′ is the axis of the smallest moment, y′ of the middle one and z′ of the largest;
    • each axis points to the positive side of the shape-model axis nearest to it;
    • the frame is right-handed (sbtk.internal.principal_axes).

    An eigenvector has no sign, so a field in principal axes must state these choices. Reversing x′, for example, changes the sign of every coefficient that is odd in x, such as C¯31. When two moments are equal (a body symmetric about an axis), the axes in their plane are not defined.

    What a rotation changes#

    A rotation mixes the 2l+1 coefficients of each degree among themselves and with no others. For fully normalized coefficients, the mixing matrix of each degree is orthogonal, so the power of each degree,

    σl2=∑m=0l(C¯lm2+S¯lm2),

    is unchanged. SBTK builds the rotation matrix of each degree from the solid harmonics of points and their rotated images (sbtk.internal.sh_rotation). It is exact to rounding and also rotates a covariance.

    Rotating the coefficients gives the same result as computing the field of the rotated body. The homogeneous field of the shape model with its vertices rotated into the principal axes equals the rotated homogeneous coefficients to 10−12 (testDifferenceInPrincipalAxes). Simulating a model whose body, components and random field are all carried into another frame also gives the rotated field, density and inertia (testChangeOfFrameEqualsSimulationInIt, Validation).

    Principal axes#

    In the principal axes the products of inertia vanish, so

    C¯21=S¯21=S¯22=0,C20=A+B−2C2MR2,C22=B−A4MR2  (unnormalized),

    and the whole degree-2 power is in C¯20 and C¯22. Degree 1 stays zero. No rotation makes the coefficients of degree 3 and higher zero: their values change but their power does not.

    Spin axes#

    The spin axes remove only the tilt of the axis of the largest moment:

    • z′ is the axis of the largest moment (the third principal axis);
    • x′ is the shape-model x-axis projected onto the plane normal to z′;
    • y′=z′×x′.

    Of all frames with z′ along that axis, this one turns the shape-model axes the least.

    The products of inertia that involve z vanish, so C¯21=S¯21=0. S¯22 is in general not zero, and the axis of the smallest moment lies at longitude

    λ22=12atan2(S¯22,C¯22).

    These are the conditions of a body-fixed frame of a body in principal-axis rotation, whose spin axis is the axis of its largest moment. The prime meridian here is the direction of the shape-model x-axis.

    Comparison with the homogeneous body#

    sbtk.plot.difference_from_homogeneous subtracts the field of the homogeneous body (density M/V) from a field. Both are taken about their own center of mass and expressed in the same axes, which belong to the field (frame argument). Degree 1 shows the center of mass minus the center of figure in those axes.

    What each frame shows:

    • Shape-model axes. Both fields keep their own C¯21, S¯21, S¯22. The differences mix two things: how far the interior turns the principal axes, and how much it changes the moments.
    • Spin axes. The field has C¯21=S¯21=0. The differences in these two terms are therefore minus the homogeneous body's own terms, and measure the tilt between the two axes of the largest moment (0.44° here). The differences in C¯22 and S¯22 contain the turn of the equatorial axes and the change in B−A.
    • Principal axes. The field has C¯21=S¯21=S¯22=0, so all of the misalignment is in the homogeneous body's terms: C¯21, S¯21 for the tilt and S¯22 for the turn (0.11° here). The difference in C¯22 is then the change in B−A.

    For small angles, a rotation ε about one principal axis gives the homogeneous body a product of inertia proportional to ε times the difference of the other two moments:

    |C¯21|≈C−A5/3MR2|εy|,|S¯21|≈C−B5/3MR2|εx|,|S¯22|≈B−A25/12MR2|εz|.

    A mass excess m at (x,y,z) adds the products of inertia −mxz, −myz and −mxy. An excess at mid-latitude, away from the z-axis, tilts the axis of the largest moment. An excess in a direction between the x′- and y′-axes turns the axes in the equatorial plane.

    In the example, (C−A)/(MR2)=0.083 and the tilt is 0.44°, which gives about 5×10−4, shared between C¯21 and S¯21 according to the direction of the tilt (2.0×10−4 and 3.9×10−4 in the principal axes): several times the 1σ of 10−4 assumed for degree 2. The plot returns the tilt and the turn as axes_deg. They, and the power of each degree, do not depend on the frame. Which frame to plot in depends on what the plot is for: the spin axes show the tilt directly in C¯21, S¯21, as a mission field would; the principal axes isolate all orientation differences in the homogeneous body's terms.

    Frames used for measured and modeled fields#

    Body-fixed frames of measured fields#

    Gravity fields from radio science, and the shape models made with them, are given in a body-fixed (cartographic) frame:

    • z is the spin pole;
    • x points to the prime meridian, which is defined by a surface feature;
    • the origin is the center of mass found from the tracking data.

    NEAR at Eros, Dawn at Vesta and Ceres, and OSIRIS-REx at Bennu all used frames of this kind. For a body in principal-axis rotation, the spin pole is the axis of the largest moment, so C¯21=S¯21=0. The prime meridian, however, has nothing to do with the mass distribution, so S¯22 is not zero.

    Example: Bennu#

    The OSIRIS-REx field of Bennu (JPL, 2019) and the Bennu shape model of the Bennu project, with the homogeneous Bennu in the same frame (documentation/tools/make_samples.m):

    • The origin is the center of mass. The field has no degree-1 terms. The centroid of the shape model (the center of figure) is 1.4 m from the origin, which itself is a sign of a non-uniform interior.
    • C¯21 and S¯21 are zero. Their values are ~10−14 with a 1σ of 8×10−10, so they were constrained to zero by taking the spin pole as the axis of the largest moment.
    • The frame is not the principal frame of the shape. The homogeneous Bennu has C¯21=4.7×10−4: its axis of the largest moment is 0.75° from the spin pole.
    • S¯22 is not zero. The measured field's axis of the smallest moment is at longitude −9.8°; the homogeneous body's is at −7.1°.

    An interior model of Bennu must therefore tilt the axis of the largest moment by 0.75° and turn the equatorial axes by 2.7°. In Cavale et al. (Icarus) this is what the boulders that target C¯21/S¯21 and C¯22/S¯22 do. Rotating the field 9.8° about z would make S¯22 zero. It would also separate the field from the frame of the shape model, the maps and the navigation, and gain nothing, since the angle is already given by S¯22/C¯22.

    Principal-axis frames#

    Principal axes are used for:

    • rotational dynamics: the equations of rotation (Euler's equations) are diagonal in them;
    • model fields made for dynamics studies before a body is visited;
    • tumbling asteroids, whose rotation state is solved in the principal axes of the body (for example 4179 Toutatis and 99942 Apophis).

    Which frame to use#

    Makers#

    The HLP6 makers agreed (20 October 2026) on the first of these two choices.

    1. The frame and origin of the shape model file, as distributed (the HLP6 choice). The package contains the shape model file unchanged. The gravity field is expanded about its origin, so degree 1 gives the center of mass and is not zero, and C¯21, S¯21 are those of the whole body in the shape-model axes (the homogeneous Didymos v003 alone has C¯21=3.7×10−3). The moments of inertia are about the same origin. Make the exact values with sbtk.data.simulate and expand them about [0 0 0] with sbtk.data.expand_about. Nothing is moved or rotated, so every team can compare with its own computation on the file as it is; a package of the homogeneous body lets the makers check that they compute the same field (Hera blind test).
    2. Moved to the center of mass and rotated (kept in SBTK; tutorial ex09). The origin is the center of mass of the true model, and the axes are - the spin axes for a body observed to rotate about one axis (Didymos): C¯21=S¯21=0 and S¯22≠0, as a mission field would look. The homogeneous Didymos v003 is already 3.6° off the shape-model z-axis, and the example true model 4.0°; - the principal axes for a body that may be tumbling, or a synchronous satellite (Dimorphos; Tumbling bodies).

    The body, the exact values and the true model are carried into that frame (code below), and the package contains the moved and rotated shape model.

    In either case, use one frame for everything you send: the measurements, the shape model file, the interior-model package and the density grid, so that the receiving teams' density grids coincide with yours. Define the interior in the frame of the shape model file.

    Points to check:

    The next three points concern the moved and rotated frame.

    • The frame of the true model is exact. A real spin pole has an error ε, which leaves |C¯21|,|S¯21|≈(C−A)ε/(5/3MR2), about 1.1×10−4 for Didymos at ε=0.1°. A package of exact values has C¯21=S¯21=0 exactly; the 1σ a receiving team gives them stands for the pole error. If that 1σ is much smaller, the data define the pole more precisely than a mission would.
    • The rotation carries information about the interior. It is the same information the field gives in the shape-model axes (C¯21, S¯21), and the same a mission's pole and field give together, no more. conventions.txt records the change of frame from the original shape-model file.
    • The origin is the center of mass. Mission body-fixed frames put the origin at the estimated center of mass (Bennu: the centroid is 1.4 m from the origin). The shape model, the gravity field and the moments of inertia then share one origin, and a receiving team has nothing to move. The origin of the original file has no physical meaning (Didymos v003: 57 m from the center of figure). Moving the origin adds no information: a package of exact values gives the exact center of mass in center_of_mass.txt anyway, together with the center of figure, and their offset, which the interior sets, is the same in every frame. For a package with noise, a mission's origin is the measured center of mass; translate to it after sbtk.data.add_noise (a translation applies to any set).
    • Uncertainties belong to a frame. A table of one σ per coefficient describes the solution it comes from, in that solution's axes. In other axes the same covariance has correlations, and two coefficients of one degree can have very different σ (in a principal-axis solution, for example, σ(C¯22)≪σ(S¯22)). Applying such a table coefficient by coefficient in other axes is an approximation. sbtk.data.to_frame rotates a full covariance exactly.
    • Components tied to the rotation. A component defined in the shape-model frame, such as a torus about its z-axis, is tilted relative to the spin axes by the angle between them. If it should lie about the spin axis, as an equatorial torus does, set its axis with sbtk.interior.align_with_spin_axis before simulating (Axes of components). interior_model.txt gives the geometry in the frame of the package.

    Finders#

    1. Work in the frame of the package. Its shape model and its measurements are in the same frame. Define the components of the inversion model in that frame.
    2. A package in the frame and about the origin of the shape model file (the HLP6 choice): fit the coefficients, degree 1 included, and the moments of inertia as they are, about the origin of the file, with no change of frame. sbtk.exchange.read_data returns them about [0 0 0] (coef_about = 'origin'), and the inversion predicts the model's coefficients and inertia about the same point. The homogeneous body already has a degree 1 (its center of figure, 57 m from the origin of the Didymos v003 file) and a C¯21, S¯21; the interior has to account for the difference, in particular the offset of the center of mass from the center of figure.
    3. If a field and its shape model are in different frames (for example a field in principal axes and a shape model in its own frame, with the rotation given), rotate the shape model with sbtk.body.to_frame, not the measurements.
    4. Report the solution and its density grid in the frame of the package, so that the cells coincide with those of the interior-model package.
    5. To interpret the orientation of the field, compare it with the homogeneous body in the spin or principal axes, and quote the angles axes_deg.

    Packages moved to the center of mass and rotated#

    MATLAB script: Measurement and interior-model packages in the spin axes
    run("/path/to/SBTK/sbtk_setup.m")
body = sbtk.body.from_mass("Didymos", sbtk.shape.bundled("didymos"), 5.273e11, 0.43);
cof = body.centroid_km;                                                      % place from the center of figure
torus = sbtk.interior.torus("torus", 0.370, 0.055, 0, -500);
torus.center_km = cof;
interior = sbtk.interior.create([sbtk.interior.sphere("core", cof, 0.170, +450), torus]);
interior = sbtk.interior.align_with_spin_axis(body, interior, "torus", 0.01, 20);   % torus about the spin axis
[exact, truth] = sbtk.data.simulate(body, interior, 4, "spin example");      % in the frame of the shape-model file

spin  = sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame(truth, body, "spin");             % or "principal"
body  = sbtk.body.to_frame(body, spin.com_km, spin.axes, "didymos_v003_com_spin_axes");   % origin: center of mass
truth = sbtk.interior.to_frame(truth, spin.com_km, spin.axes);
exact = sbtk.data.to_frame(exact, spin.com_km, spin.axes);                   % C21 = S21 = 0

folder = tempname;                                                           % exact values: no uncertainties, no noise
data  = sbtk.exchange.write_data(folder, "DOC-DATA9-Didymos", body, exact, "Example team");
model = sbtk.exchange.write_model(folder, "DOC-MOD9-Didymos", "DOC-DATA9-Didymos", body, truth, exact, 0.040, ...
                                  "Example team");
[received, rbody] = sbtk.exchange.read_data(data);                           % what a receiving team reads
fprintf("%s: C21 %+.1e, S21 %+.1e\n", rbody.shape.name, received.coef(5:6))
unc = sbtk.data.uncertainties([sbtk.units.offset_to_degree1(0.002, 0.43), 1e-4, 2e-4, 4e-4], 2e-3, 0.02);
received = sbtk.data.set_uncertainties(received, unc);                       % the receiving team's uncertainties

    The package contains DOC-DATA9-Didymos_didymos_v003_com_spin_axes.obj, named after the package because no other package has this shape model: the DART shape model with every vertex moved to the center of mass of the true model and rotated into its spin axes, x′=(x−c)A with c the center of mass and the columns of A the spin axes, both in the frame of the original file. The center of mass is at the origin of that file, and the gravity field, the moments of inertia and every position of both packages are in its frame, so a receiving team uses it as it is. Its conventions.txt states what the origin and the axes are (read from the values: the center of mass at the origin, C¯21=S¯21=0) and the change of frame from the original file. interior_model.txt describes the torus with its axis in the new frame: along z, because it was aligned with the spin axis. The package has exact values without uncertainties (Packages of exact values). The HLP6 runners have a FRAME setting that does this (Hera blind test).

    Tumbling bodies and Dimorphos#

    The gravity field does not depend on the rotation. The coefficients and the principal axes describe the mass distribution in a body-fixed frame and do not change with the rotation state.

    What changes for a tumbling body is the meaning of "spin axis". In principal-axis rotation the body spins about its axis of the largest moment. A tumbling body (non-principal-axis rotation) has angular momentum fixed in space, but its angular velocity moves through the body, so no body-fixed axis is the spin axis. The rotation is described in the principal axes, in which Euler's equations are diagonal.

    Dimorphos. Before the DART impact, Dimorphos was taken to rotate synchronously with its orbit about Didymos. In that state the body-fixed frame (z the orbit normal, x toward Didymos on average) is close to its principal axes. The impact changed its orbit, and simulations (Agrusa et al., 2021, Icarus) showed that it could have excited non-principal-axis rotation. Its present rotation state is not known; Hera will measure it. The DART shape model v004 is from before the impact, and the impact may have changed the shape.

    For making and finding data sets this means:

    • Frame. Use the principal axes. They suit a tumbling body and, within a few degrees, a synchronous one. The spin axes remain a valid body-fixed frame for a tumbler, but their z-axis is no longer a spin axis.
    • What the rotation measures.
    • A librating synchronous body gives the libration ratio (B−A)/C (Moments of inertia).
    • A tumbling body gives two ratios of principal moments, for example A/C and B/C, because Euler's equations depend only on ratios. Together with the degree-2 field, which fixes B−A and C−(A+B)/2 in units of MR2, two ratios fix all three moments, including the trace that the gravity field cannot give.

    A tumbling Dimorphos would therefore constrain the radial density structure better than a librating one. - Moment-ratio measurements in SBTK. In principal axes the ratios are the linear rows Ixx−rAIzz=0 and Iyy−rBIzz=0, with σ = σrIzz, added with sbtk.data.add_moi_row. In other axes the weights follow from the principal axes of the degree-2 field. The exchange format gives inertia elements and the libration ratio but not moment ratios. A group can give Ixx, Iyy, Izz in principal axes, which are A, B, C, or agree on an extension of the format. - The interior model is made as before. The true interior is defined in the shape-model frame and carried into the principal axes; the rotation state affects only the rotation measurements. Finders invert as usual in the frame of the package.

    MATLAB script: Ratios of principal moments of a tumbling Dimorphos
    run("/path/to/SBTK/sbtk_setup.m")
body = sbtk.body.from_mass("Dimorphos", sbtk.shape.bundled("dimorphos"), 2.700e9, 0.10);
interior = sbtk.interior.create(sbtk.interior.ellipsoid("core", body.centroid_km + [0.004 0 0], [0.045 0.040 0.028], ...
                                                        [15 0 0], +400));
[exact, truth] = sbtk.data.simulate(body, interior, 3, "tumbling example");
cp = sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame(truth, body, "principal");
exact = sbtk.data.to_frame(exact, [0 0 0], cp.axes);           % Ixx = A, Iyy = B, Izz = C
I = exact.moi_tensor_norm;
rA = I(1, 1)/I(3, 3);  rB = I(2, 2)/I(3, 3);  sigma_r = 0.005;
obs = sbtk.data.select_moi(exact, strings(1, 0));
obs = sbtk.data.add_moi_row(obs, "A_over_C", [1 0 -rA 0 0 0], 0, sigma_r*I(3, 3));
obs = sbtk.data.add_moi_row(obs, "B_over_C", [0 1 -rB 0 0 0], 0, sigma_r*I(3, 3));
fprintf("A/C = %.4f, B/C = %.4f; off-diagonal elements in principal axes: %.1e\n", rA, rB, max(abs(I(~eye(3)))))
