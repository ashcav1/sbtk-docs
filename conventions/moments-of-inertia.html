    Moments of inertia#

    Inertia tensor#

    The inertia tensor about the expansion origin, in the axes of the shape-model frame, is

    Iij=∫ρ(r2δij−xixj)dV,

    so the products of inertia carry the minus sign: Ixy=−∫ρxydV. SBTK stores the six independent elements in the order [Ixx,Iyy,Izz,Ixy,Ixz,Iyz], normalized by MRref2 in measurement sets and models (truth.inertia_norm, fm.inertia_norm) and in kg m² in reports (Units).

    Relation to the degree-2 coefficients#

    About the center of mass, in the same axes and with the same Rref, the unnormalized degree-2 coefficients are

    C20=Ixx+Iyy−2Izz2MR2,C21=−IxzMR2,S21=−IyzMR2,C22=Iyy−Ixx4MR2,S22=−Ixy2MR2,

    and C¯lm=Clm/Nlm (Gravity coefficients). These relations are verified by testDegreeTwoAndInertia (Validation).

    The degree-2 field fixes five combinations of the six elements. The sixth, the trace (for example through the polar moment Izz), is the information that a moment-of-inertia measurement adds to the gravity field: it measures how strongly the mass is concentrated toward the center, which the gravity coefficients alone cannot distinguish from other radial distributions of the same field. Tutorial ex08 compares inversions with different inertia measurements.

    Measurement rows#

    A moment-of-inertia measurement is one linear combination of the elements:

    𝐰·[Ixx,Iyy,Izz,Ixy,Ixz,Iyz]MRref2=v±σ,

    stored as a moi row with weights 𝐰, value v and sigma σ. A single element has one weight equal to 1 (sbtk.data.add_moi); any other combination is added with sbtk.data.add_moi_row. sbtk.data.select_moi chooses the rows a data set contains and opts.moi_labels (opts.moi_labels) the rows an inversion fits.

    Libration ratio#

    The libration ratio is

    k=B−AC,

    with A≤B the principal moments of inertia about the axes in the xy-plane and C=Izz; the spin axis z is taken as a principal axis, which holds exactly in the spin or principal axes of the field (Frames of a gravity field). For a tumbling body the rotation gives ratios of principal moments instead (Tumbling bodies). With a the angle of those principal axes from the x-axis,

    B−A=cos(2a)(Iyy−Ixx)+sin(2a)·2Ixy,

    which is linear in the tensor once a is fixed. sbtk.data.add_libration takes a from the measured C¯22 and S¯22, since (cos(2a),sin(2a)) is proportional to (C22,−S22) about the center of mass, and adds the linear row

    cos(2a)(Iyy−Ixx)+sin(2a)·2Ixy−kIzz=0±σkIzz,

    labeled libration, with weights [−cos(2a),cos(2a),−k,2sin(2a),0,0] (sbtk.internal.libration_weights). Together with C22 and S22, which give B−A, the ratio measures C with a relative precision of about σk/k. If C22=S22=0 the principal axes in the xy-plane are undefined and the function stops with sbtk:data:axisymmetric.

    Libration ratio from a libration amplitude#

    For a synchronously rotating satellite in a Keplerian orbit of eccentricity e about a point-mass primary, the forced libration angle γ of the long axis from the direction to the primary obeys, to first order in e,

    γ¨+3n2kγ=2en2sinM,

    with mean motion n and mean anomaly M, so γ=GsinM with

    G=2e3k−1,k=13(1+2eG).

    sbtk.data.libration_ratio_from_amplitude returns k and its 1σ from G, e and their uncertainties by first-order error propagation. G<0 for k<1/3 (elongations up to about 1.4 : 1). The sensitivity of G to k grows near k=1/3, where the free libration period equals the orbital period. Free libration, the primary's oblateness and the mutual dynamics of a binary are not included.
