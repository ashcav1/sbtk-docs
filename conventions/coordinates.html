    Coordinate systems and origins#

    Shape-model frame#

    Every position in SBTK is given in the shape-model frame: the origin and axes of the shape model in use, lengths in km. The z-axis is taken as the spin axis. Measurements, models and estimates of one body are in the same frame. sbtk.body.to_frame, sbtk.data.to_frame and sbtk.interior.to_frame carry a body, its measurements and its interior into another frame, for example the spin or principal axes of a true model, which then becomes the shape-model frame of everything that follows; sbtk.gravity.center_of_mass_frame expresses a gravity field in the shape-model, spin or principal axes (Frames of a gravity field).

    Three points of the shape-model frame are distinct in general:

    Point Definition Where SBTK stores it
    Shape-model origin The point (0, 0, 0) of the shape file, chosen by its makers. For the DART Didymos model v003 it is 57 m from the center of figure; for the OSIRIS-REx Bennu model it is the center of mass found from the tracking data, 1.4 m from the center of figure. the origin of every coordinate
    Center of figure Centroid of the volume enclosed by the shape model: the center of mass of a homogeneous body. body.centroid_km, props.centroid_km
    Center of mass Center of mass of the density model. It differs from the center of figure when the density is not uniform. truth.com_km, obs.com_minus_cof_km, center_of_mass.txt of a measurement package

    Expansion origin#

    Gravity coefficients, first moments and the inertia tensor are computed about a point, the expansion origin. It is stored as origin_km in measurement sets (obs.origin_km), true models (truth.origin_km), inversion results (fit.origin_km) and geometries (geo.origin_km).

    • sbtk.data.simulate expands about the true center of mass of the simulated interior. Because layers and sectors are measured from the expansion origin, the center of mass depends on the origin; the simulation repeats the calculation about the new center of mass until it moves by less than 1 mm.
    • Measurement packages read with sbtk.exchange.read_data are expanded about the measured center of mass, and the inversion moves its predictions to the model's own center of mass (Density estimation).
    • sbtk.gravity.geometry takes any expansion origin about which the shape is star-shaped.

    Positions of components#

    Components are positioned in two different ways:

    • Spheres, ellipsoids and tori are placed in shape-model coordinates. center_km = [0 0 0] is the shape-model origin, not the center of figure. A torus lies about its axis, the third of component.axes, through component.center_km, in the plane at height_km along that axis; as constructed this is the z-axis through the origin. To place components from the center of figure, as recommended, use body.centroid_km: a sphere at body.centroid_km + offset, a torus with center_km = body.centroid_km (Where to place components).
    • Layers and sectors are measured along rays from the expansion origin: a layer's depth is the distance below the surface along the ray through the point; a sector's radial fraction is the distance from the origin divided by the distance from the origin to the surface along the same ray, and its latitude and longitude are measured about the origin.

    Latitude and longitude#

    For a vector 𝐱=(x,y,z) from the point of reference, latitude ϕ is measured from the xy-plane toward +z and longitude λ from +x toward +y:

    𝐱=d(cosϕcosλ, cosϕsinλ, sinϕ).

    sbtk.interior.latlon returns 𝐱 from ϕ, λ (degrees) and d (km). Sector longitudes are compared in [−180°,180°) with ranges that may extend beyond ±180°; for example [150°,240°] includes −170°.

    Ellipsoid orientation#

    An ellipsoid's semi-axes (a,b,c) lie along x, y, z before rotation. The rotation is R=Rz(ψ)Ry(θ)Rx(φ) with ψ the yaw (about z), θ the pitch (about y) and φ the roll (about x), given in degrees as [yaw pitch roll]. The columns of R (component.axes) are the ellipsoid's axes in the shape-model frame, and a point 𝐱 is inside when ∑i([R𝖳(𝐱−𝐜)]i/ai)2<1.

    Axes of the inertia tensor and the coefficients#

    Coefficients and inertia elements are given about the center of mass but in the axes of the shape-model frame, not in principal axes of inertia, so C¯21, S¯21 and S¯22 are in general not zero. For a homogeneous body, the axis of the largest moment of inertia is 3.6° from the shape-model z-axis for Didymos (DART v003) and 0.7° for Dimorphos (v004), and the principal axes in the xy-plane are 33.6° and 7.8° from x; in the shape-model axes the homogeneous Didymos has C¯21=3.7×10−3, S¯21=8.8×10−4 and S¯22=−7.0×10−3. In the spin axes of a field (z along its axis of the largest moment) C¯21=S¯21=0, and in its principal axes also S¯22=0; the interior-model package states the principal axes of the true model (Frames of a gravity field). A field and its shape model must be in the same frame: when they are not, carry the shape model into the frame of the field with sbtk.body.to_frame. The libration ratio uses the principal moments in the xy-plane computed from the tensor (Moments of inertia), and takes z as a principal axis, which holds exactly in the spin and principal axes.
