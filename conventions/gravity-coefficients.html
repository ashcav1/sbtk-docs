    Gravity coefficients#

    Last edited October 20, 2026

    Expansion of the potential#

    Outside the smallest sphere about the expansion origin that encloses the body (the Brillouin sphere), the gravitational potential is

    U(r,ϕ,λ)=GMr∑l=0L(Rrefr)l∑m=0lP¯lm(sinϕ)[C¯lmcos(mλ)+S¯lmsin(mλ)]

    with r, latitude ϕ and longitude λ measured from the expansion origin in the axes of the shape-model frame (Coordinate systems). U>0 and the acceleration is +∇U. C¯00=1. Rref is a chosen reference radius; SBTK computes the coefficients and does not evaluate the series.

    For a density ρ(𝐱) of total mass M the fully normalized coefficients are

    C¯lm=1(2l+1)M∫ρ(rRref)lP¯lm(sinϕ)cos(mλ)dV, S¯lm=1(2l+1)M∫ρ(rRref)lP¯lm(sinϕ)sin(mλ)dV.

    Normalization#

    The coefficients are fully normalized (the 4π normalization of geodesy) and the associated Legendre functions have no Condon–Shortley phase (−1)m:

    P¯lm=NlmPlm,Nlm=(2−δm0)(2l+1)(l−m)!(l+m)!,Plm(x)=(1−x2)m/2dmdxmPl(x),

    so that the mean of [P¯lm(sinϕ)cos(mλ)]2 over the sphere is 1. Unnormalized coefficients are Clm=NlmC¯lm. Examples: N20=5, N21=5/3, N22=5/12, N30=7. The zonal coefficients are Jl=−Cl0=−2l+1C¯l0.

    Coefficients in another convention must be converted before use: multiply by (−1)m to remove a Condon–Shortley phase, and by 1/Nlm to normalize. sbtk.exchange.read_shadr checks the normalization state in the file header and rejects unnormalized files; sbtk.io.read_jpl_gravity converts Jl to C¯l0.

    Reference radius#

    Changing the reference radius from R to R′ multiplies a coefficient of degree l by (R/R′)l; its variance by (R/R′)2l. sbtk.data.use_uncertainties applies this when it takes uncertainties from a data set with another reference radius. The examples on this site use Rref=0.43 km for Didymos and 0.10 km for Dimorphos.

    Order of coefficients#

    Vectors of coefficients are ordered by degree, and within a degree as Cl0, then Clm,Slm for m=1…l:

    C00, C10, C11, S11, C20, C21, S21, C22, S22, C30, C31, S31, …

    sbtk.gravity.labels returns the names to a given degree and pk describes the order. Measurement sets (obs) leave out C00 and store the names in obs.coef_labels. Where coefficients are given as matrices (sbtk.data.from_coefficients), C(l+1, m+1) holds C¯lm and S(l+1, m+1) holds S¯lm.

    Degree 1 and the center of mass#

    About an expansion origin O, with (xc,yc,zc) the center of mass minus O,

    C¯10=zc3Rref,C¯11=xc3Rref,S¯11=yc3Rref.

    About the center of mass these vanish. A measured center of mass enters a measurement set as these three coefficients with their uncertainties (sbtk.data.add_center_of_mass, sbtk.units.offset_to_degree1).

    Frame of the coefficients: coef_about#

    Each measurement set states what its coefficients are about (obs.coef_about):

    • 'origin': every coefficient, degree 1 included, and the inertia are about obs.origin_km. Simulated measurements are of this kind, with the origin at the true center of mass; sbtk.data.expand_about expands them about another point, for example [0 0 0], the origin of the shape model file. Packages about the origin of their shape model file, as the HLP6 makers send, are read as this kind.
    • 'center_of_mass': the coefficients of degree 2 and higher are about the body's center of mass, as a radio-science solution is; the degree-1 coefficients give the measured position of the center of mass relative to obs.origin_km. Packages about the center of mass and fields given to sbtk.data.add_center_of_mass are of this kind.

    The inversion treats both as described in Density estimation. sbtk.data.move_origin changes the origin of 'center_of_mass' data; only the degree-1 values change.
